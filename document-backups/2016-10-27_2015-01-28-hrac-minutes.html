4. SIGN/FAÇADE APPLICATIONS 
A. Vincent reviewed the role of HRAC in consideration of sign and façade applications. As per 
Section 3.2.2 of the Sign & Canopy Bylaw 1176, property located in the Downtown Specified 
Area must be referred to HRAC for consideration on colour, design, location, and style. 
 
a. 49th Parallel Grocery (Har-Way Holdings) – 1020 First Avenue 
P. Richmond of the 49 th Parallel Grocery presented the fascia and free -standing signs 
proposed for 1020 First Avenue. Members agreed that the style and location of the 
fascia sign was a good fit with the buildings.  
 
It was moved, seconded and carried that the Heritage Revitalization Advisory  Commission 
recommend the fascia sign proposal as presented for Har -Way Holdings/49 th Parallel Grocery at 
1020 First Avenue. 
 
Members discussed the free-standing sign with the following comments:  
 Consideration of whether the sign was too large, but that a larger sign would 
be necessary for multiple tenants. 
 Preference for the free-standing cedar sign located at 49th Parallel Grocery in 
Cedar. 
 Generally positive regarding the design and location. 
 
It was moved, seconded and carried that the Heritage Revitalization Advisory  Commission 
recommend the free -standing sign proposal as presented for Har -Way Holdings/49 th Parallel 
Grocery at 1020 First Avenue.

b. Oceans Seafood Restaurant – 431 First Avenue 
Members reviewed  the proposed sign and facade and provided the following 
suggestions: 
 The sign and lettering could be larger to be more legible. 
 Interest in retaining awnings on the building since these are both useful and add 
to the character of Ladysmith’s downtown.  
 
It was moved, seconded and carried that the Heritage Revitalization Advisory  Commission 
recommend the facade proposal for Oceans Seafood Restaurant at 431 First Avenue. 
 
5. HERITAGE PROJECT UPDATES 
 a.   2014 Statements of Significance – Commissioner Review 
Members discussed the Statements of Significance, provided at the December 2014 
HRAC meeting, and had no further comments. A. Vincent described the subsequent 
steps of the process in recognizing the heritage properties on the Ladysmith Community 
Heritage Register and Provincial Heritage Register. 
 
b. Heritage Week 
A summary was provided of HRAC Heritage Week initiatives, including a n ad in Take5 
Magazine, the Heritage Week coro plast sign displayed on Esplanade, and extensive 
research and archival photographs provided by the Ladysmith & District Historical 
Society for posters, plus graphic design and printing costs.  
 
Members discussed potential locations to display the eight sets of posters, including: 
 FJCC 
 Aggie Hall 
 City Hall 
 LRCA/Senior’s Centre 
 Library 
 49th Parallel Grocery 
 516 First Avenue 
 Coronation Mall 
 
M. Martin will contact locations regarding displaying the posters. A. McDermid will 
display posters after delivery on February 13, 2015. 
 
Other initiatives for Heritage Week include a n event organized by Take5, to be held on 
February 1 8, 2015. The evening includes a presentation and slideshow of archival 
photographs. Take5 Magazine will run Heritage Week information , and the Chronicle 
Newspaper will publish an article by Ed Nicholson. 
 
Members discussed options for preparing for future Heritage Weeks, including 
contacting local businesses and organizations throughout the year (prior to learning the 
Heritage Week theme) to ensure awareness as an annual event. 
 
A banner to be displayed above First Avenue will be discussed further next year. 
 
c. Aggie Hall Plaque 
Members reviewed the design for the Aggie Hall plaque and provided positive feedback. 
 
d. Transfer Beach Pictorial 
A. Vincent announced that Heritage BC had awarded the Town of Ladysmith a $4,000 
Heritage Legacy Fund grant towards the Transfer Beach Pictorial.
