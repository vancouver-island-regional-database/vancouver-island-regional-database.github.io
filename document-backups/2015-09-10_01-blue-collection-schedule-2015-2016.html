Frequently Asked Questions 
 
How can my family of six produce only one can of garbage  
every other week? 
By composting and recycling you will reduce the garbage your household 
sends to the landfill by 90%. 
 
How much garbage can I put out on my collection day? 
The maximum total weight per garbage  container is 20kg (44lbs) including 
container.  The limit ensures operators can lift without risk of injury.  This is 
approximately one standard 70L (18.5gal) garbage can. Please note that 
some garbage cans sold are larger than 70L and if the larger can is filled to 
capacity it may be over weight and won’t be picked up.   
R
emember you can put out unlimited amounts of recyclables and  
organics in the proper containers. 
 
What if I have more than 70L of garbage? 
You can buy additional garbage tickets for $2.00 at City Hall and Frank 
Jameson Community Centre. 
 
What can be used to line my organic bins? 
Lining your organic bins is optional, but if you choose to do so, compostable 
bags which are commonly made with corn starch or sugar cane fibre can be 
used.  You can buy these at most local businesses that sell regular garbage 
bags.  You can also use newspaper.  B
iodegradable (ie: shopping) bags are 
NOT accepted because they do not break down into organic matter. 
 
Where can I get yellow recycling bags and recycle stickers? 
City Hall and Frank Jameson Community Centre have yellow bags for  
purchase ($1.00) and recycle stickers free of charge.   
 
What is the phone number for Peerless Road Recycling and  
Drop-off  Depot?  250.245.7800 
Peerless Road is a facility operated by the Cowichan Valley Regional  
District.  The hours of operation are: 
April 1st to October 31st, Wed through Sun,  9:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.  
November 1st to March 31st,  Wed, Sat & Sun, 9:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.   
CVRD Recycling Hotline at 1.800.665.3955.

 WASTE  REDUCTION :  
The Town of Ladysmith is a leader in sustainability: 
in 1995 the Town was first in the CVRD to introduce  
     curb-side recycling pick up 
in 2006 Ladysmith was the first community in Western  
     Canada to provide curb-side organics pick up   
 
The Town was recognized in 2006 with the Union of British  
Columbia Municipalities Community Excellence Award in the 
Best Practices Category for the program and in 2007 with the 
Federation of Canadian Municipalities Sustainable Communi-
ties Award in the Solid Waste Category . 
 
Before the recycling and curb -side organics programs, 100% of 
our household waste went into a landfill.  By 2009, the recycling 
program was diverting 33% and the organics program a further 
27%.  
 
Residents, take a moment to applaud your efforts in  
recycling or composting 60% of our Town’s garbage! 
 
Did you know that your household waste is made up of 90% 
recyclable or compostable material?  That means 30% of what 
we still put in our garbage cans could also be diverted and used 
as a resource.   
 
So what does this mean to you?  Besides the obvious benefits 
to our environment and quality of life, it means 
MONEY.  Our garbage currently goes to Washington 
State and shipping is costly.  The less garbage we 
send the less you as a taxpayer spend. 
 
ZERO WASTE is rethinking how we view waste. Ra-
ther than  worthless, to alternatively think of it as a resource, a 
beneficial commodity for reuse by others, and importantly, to 
not merely recycle, but put emphasis on  
reducing the amount of resources we consume.
