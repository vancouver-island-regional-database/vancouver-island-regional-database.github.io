REPORT ON REMEDIATION COSTS FOR LADYSMITH WATERFRONT RELEASED 
 
LADYSMITH, November 27, 2012 – The Province of B.C. and the Council of the Town 
of Ladysmith have released a long anticipated report on the environmental condition 
of the Ladysmith waterfront. The report outlines the extent of environmental 
contamination and gives a range of costs for alternatives to address remediation.  
 
Decades of industrial activity in the Ladysmith harbour, including; a copper smelter, 
coal washing, log sorting, and railway maintenance and repair have left a legacy of 
contamination.  Golder Associates Ltd. was hired by the provincial government to 
carry out site investigations, sediment testing and geotechnical investigations on the 
Provincial Water Lots and Town lands.  The testing identified the type and likely 
amount of contamination in various locations along the Ladysmith waterfront 
between Fisherman’s Wharf in the north and Transfer Beach in the south.  With that 
information in hand, the consultants were able to provide a range of cost estimates 
for remediating the contamination.  
 
Costs for remediation would be largely determined by the type of development, and 
are currently estimated at $26.8 million for the development options considered for 
the upland, filled foreshore and marine areas.  These costs do not include the costs 
for addressing the geotechnical stabilization of Slack Point or erosion control along 
the coastline.  
 
“The environmental remediation of the waterfront area is priority for the Town of 
Ladysmith and the Stz’uminus First Nation,” said Mayor Rob Hutchins.  “We 
appreciate the financial commitment of the Province to undertake this
