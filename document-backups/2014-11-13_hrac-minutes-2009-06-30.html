B. Bancroft presented a rationale for the proposed colours for 30 Roberts Street and 
411 First Avenue. The proposed colours are Benjamin Moore: Golden Nugget 2019-
20, Venezuelan Sea 2054-30, Flame 2012-20, and Buckhorn CC-510. B. Bancroft 
suggests that the west side of First Avenue draws the majority of the attention and 
customers partly due to the attractive heritage buildings, while the east side tends to 
be uninteresting and dull in colour. The bright colours proposed for 411 First Avenue 
are intended to anchor the corner building, attract attention to the east side of the 
street and provide vibrancy and energy.  The Buckhorn colour will be at the base of 
the building to allow for a calm pedestrian experience at street level.  
B. Bancroft suggests that the existing colours of buildings in town are too dull, faded 
and blend with one another, (with the exception of a few buildings on Roberts Street), 
and it is his hope that the proposed co lours will start a new trend in downtown 
Ladysmith.  
B. Bancroft explains that the canopy at 411 First Avenue will be replaced with a 
flatter style canopy similar to the Ladysmith Trading Co. canopy.  
HRAC members discussed the proposed colours for the two buildings.  
It was moved, seconded and carried that the Heritage Revitalization Advisory Committee 
recommends approval of the following Benjamin Moore façade colours: 30 Roberts Street – Flame 
2012-20, Venezuelan Sea 2054-30, Golden Nugget 2019-20; and 411 First Avenue -  Golden 
Nugget 2019-20, Venezuelan Sea 2054-30, Flame 2012-20, and Buckhorn CC-510.

c. Logo “A View To Sea” 
Councillor, L. Evans explained that the logo “A View To Sea” is part of economic 
development tourism advertising and has been such for approximately 2 years. She 
advised that the Heritage Tourism Workshop Report will be forwarded to the 
Economic Development Commission. 
 
Mayor, R. Hutchins explained that the Town has two logos “Heritage By The Sea” and 
“A View To Sea”.

HRAC members discussed this matter and the following concerns were raised: 
• Two logos for a town of this size is not an ideal marketing approach and 
sends confusing messages; and 
• The logo “A View To Sea” is not unique for an island town whereas the logo 
“Heritage By The Sea” is a better reflection of the Town of Ladysmith. 
 
HRAC members requested that their concern regarding the logo “A View To Sea” be 
referred to the Economic Development Commission and Government Services 
Committee for consideration.
