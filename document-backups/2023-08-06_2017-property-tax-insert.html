In 2017, we have been able to hold the line on 
property tax increases while making signiﬁ cant 
investments in the future of our community, laying a 
foundation for sustainable growth. 
In fact, this year there is a reduction of about 0.4 
per cent in property taxes based on assessed values.  
For an average single family dwelling, the municipal 
share of property tax you pay will actually go down 
by $5.30 compared to last year.  
Ladysmith’s total municipal taxation for 2017 is 
$7.733 million.  This helps pay for the ongoing costs 
of maintaining parks, roads and other infrastructure, 
keeping our community safe, running the Frank 
Jameson Community Centre, operating our 
sewer and water systems, supporting many local 
community organizations, and more.
There are a number of signiﬁ cant, generational 
projects underway or planned for our community in 
2017.  These include:
• Starting construction on the new Water 
Filtration Plant
• Building the new bio-solids composting facility
• Expanding the truck bays at the Fire Hall to 
accommodate a new ﬁ re rescue vehicle
• Expanding parking and improving the 
playground at Aggie Hall 
• Finishing the Waterfront Area Plan Update 
• Setting aside funds to build a reserve for the 
replacement of aging infrastructure, including 
City Hall and the Fire Hall
We are very fortunate to have received signiﬁ cant 
grants to help offset some of our capital costs and 
reduce the burden on our taxpayers. We continue to 
apply for every opportunity.
The full budget and related documents are available 
on our website at www.ladysmith.ca, and at City 
Hall. We encourage you to review them and direct 
comments or questions to Council or 
staff.

WHA T SERVICES DO YOUR PROPERTY T AXES PA Y FOR?
Each dollar that you pay in property taxes funds a large number of programs and services -- to keep 
Ladysmith clean and safe, repair roads and sidewalks, maintain parks and trails, and support community 
groups and organizations. The chart below shows how one dollar of your property taxes is spent. 
In addition to the amounts listed here, property owners also pay water and sewer parcel taxes to help 
fund our water supply system, the new water ﬁ ltration plant, and our waste water treatment plant. 
Our biggest capital expenditure in 2017 will be building a new water ﬁ ltration plant. This is required by 
Island Health (VIHA) in order for our water to meet quality standards for drinking water and to remove 
chlorination byproducts from our water supply. The plant is estimated to cost $14 million, with funds 
coming from water parcel taxes, borrowing and a federal/provincial grant of $8.8 million.  We would like 
to take this opportunity to thank the community for supporting this important initiative through the 
recent Alternative Approval Process.

DID YOU KNOW?
35% of all property taxes collected by the T own of 
Ladysmith go directly to other agencies to pay for 
services like schools, the Vancouver Island Regional 
Library, the Cowichan Valley Regional District and 
the Cowichan Regional Hospital fund. Sewer and 
water parcel taxes make up 18 per cent of taxes 
collected.
