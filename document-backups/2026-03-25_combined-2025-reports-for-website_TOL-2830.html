Please provide a description of how the funds were used to support your
event/project/service:
The funds provided through the Town of Ladysmith's Grant in Aid fund supported our efforts to
care for the biodiversity of the Holland Creek watershed and its connected areas. 2025 marked
an active and rewarding first operational year for Friends of Holland Creek (FoHC) as a growing
volunteer organization, with projects that contributed to both ecological health and community
connection. Our membership has doubled from 5 to 10, and our mailing list now includes
approximately 60 people.
The following highlights key milestones from FoHC's operations:
• FoHC developed and signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Town of
Ladysmith to outline expectations for our work in Town Parks.
• Volunteers participated in monthly gatherings to care for the Transfer Beach Pollinator
Gardens (installed in late 2024). These maintenance events are critical during the early
years of plant establishment. Activities included soil care, weed removal, transplanting
native species, and seed collection. Seed propagation has also supported ecosystem
restoration in Holland Creek Park, enhancing species diversity. The Transfer Beach
gardens are flourishing.
• FoHC hosted thirteen invasive plant removal events, targeting noxious weeds and
invasive plants such as Scotch broom, Himalayan blackberry, English ivy, and yellow
archangel. In areas where invasive plants were successfully removed, native
plants—both purchased and propagated—were introduced to help re-establish native
plant communities in disturbed portions of Holland Creek. This work will be ongoing for
several years.
The Town of Ladysmith Grant in Aid funds were primarily used for society running and
administration costs and outreach activities. These costs included We obtained other grants to
pay for web development and project costs like equipment (e.g., hand tools, gloves, tarps) and
native plants for our invasive plant removal and restoration activities in Holland Creek and the
Transfer Beach gardens. The funding also supported school programming for events at Transfer
Beach and in Holland Creek.
We are grateful for the in-kind contributions the Town of Ladysmith Operations staff have
provided by promptly removing the piles of invasive plants we've gathered, supplied soil, mulch,
and protective fencing for our restoration near the Dogwood Drive parking area.

Please describe how the community benefited from your event, project or service:
In 2025, FoHC organized a range of community activities, including:
• Family scavenger hunt increased awareness of garden escapees and invasives in
Holland Creek
• Holland Creek Biobtitz engaged residents in a community science event to collect plants
and wildlife data in Holland Creek and get to know their local ecosystem.
• Painted Lady butterfly release at the Transfer Beach garden taught the participants
about the local pollinators and inspired others to get involved and consider supporting
pollinators in their yards and public spaces.
• Presentations to the Ladysmith/Saltaire garden club and VI Master Gardeners also
promoted the use of native plants.
• A virtual learning session with the Coastal Invasive Species Committee on the high
priority invasive plants in Ladysmith provided information to empower residents to
control the spread of invasives.
• Educational event at Frank Jameson teaching participants how to use the community
science mobile app iNaturalist which is used to monitor the biodiversity in Holland Creek
and enables early detection of invasive species like the Blue Weed which could be
eradicated before it becomes widespread.
• Public meeting to get input on our future direction which identified baseline surveys as a
high priority.
These initiatives benefit the community by fostering environmental stewardship, strengthening
ecological resilience, and creating meaningful opportunities for residents to connect with nature
and each other. We had a core of between 5 and 15 volunteers at the workbees and up to
40-50 participants at the school events and Bioblitz. We formed relationships with 4-5 other non
profit groups in Ladysmith and held joint events and supported their events, for example
Ladysmith Arts Council, Ladysmith Sportsman Club, Ladysmith and Saltair Garden Club.
