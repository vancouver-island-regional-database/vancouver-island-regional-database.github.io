June 5, 2014 
 
The Cowichan Watershed Board would like to invite all Cowichan Valley and area water districts to join 
in the Water Conservation Challenge. 
 
The Town of Ladysmith in the North of our region claims to have the high ground in regards to domestic 
water conservation.  Through a combination of public education, frequent billing, and inclined block 
pricing they have managed to achieve what may be some of the lowest per capita water consumption 
numbers in Canada.  Based on single family dwellings only, and an average of 2.3 people per dwelling 
they are using 246 litres per person per day. 
 
While their numbers are very good compared to the average Canadian who uses approximately 325 
litres per day, even they fall far short of the Europeans. Water in Canada is very cheap and we waste it, 
while in Europe it is much more valued and apparently treated as the precious resource that it is. In 
Sweden, the average person consumes a mere 200 litres per day, and in Germany the average is barely 
125 litres per day. 
 
Rob Hutchins, the Mayor of Ladysmith has kindly agreed to share Ladysmith’s pricing and consumption 
data with the greater Cowichan Valley.  It may be in part due to the fact that Mayor Rob Hutchins (who 
is also Co-Chair of the Watershed Board, and Chair of the CVRD) likes to brag about his amazing little 
town, but it also provides insight into the value of letting people know when they are water wasters and 
reminding them with regular bills that reward conservation.  The bar has been set by Ladysmith, and for 
any willing to take up the challenge, now is the opportunity blow them out of the water;… in the nicest 
possible way. 
 
There are many good reasons to reduce water consumption apart from just beating Ladysmith.  It costs 
a lot of money to bring new water sources on line and to increase water main capacity.  There are real 
costs for every litre that must be pumped and treated, and costs also add up for all of the water that 
must be further treated as grey water or black water and pumped into treatment plants and septic fields 
that have maximum capacities and life spans. 
 
The Cowichan Watershed Board looks forward to generating public recognition for water districts that 
demonstrate the greatest reductions. We plan to sponsor a Black Tie Award based on progress reflected 
in annual reporting, and we will pursue media publicity.  We will be making the rounds this month and 
next with presentations to councils and water districts to share the data and other information that we 
have. We hope to put a list of challengers together and make sure everyone is kept informed as to how 
they compare “water wise” with their neighbours.
