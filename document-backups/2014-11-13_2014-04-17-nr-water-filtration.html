WATER FILTRATION HAS BEEN RECOMMENDED AS A PROACTIVE MEASURE TO 
ENSURE CONTINUED SAFE DRINKING WATER FOR LADYSMITH 
 
LADYSMITH, April 17, 2014 – A recommendation that the Town of Ladysmith install a 
secondary water treatment system is a proactive move to ensure the continued 
safety of its water supply.   
 
The recommendation was contained in a staff report to Council summarizing the 
findings of the Town’s latest annual Water Report. 
 
Ladysmith’s water supply comes from two main sources --  the Holland Lake and 
Holland Creek system, and Stocking Lake.  This is considered to be ‘surface water’ – 
as opposed to ‘ground water’, which is drawn from underground aquifers.  Water 
from both systems is treated by chlorination, collected in the Arbutus Reservoir and 
gravity-fed to the town as well as to the Diamond Improvement District. The quality of 
Ladysmith’s water supply is regulated and monitored by Island Health’s Drinking 
Water Officer.  In order to continue to hold a valid licence to supply drinking water, 
the Town must conduct – and pass -- rigorous testing as required by Island Health.  
Tests are taken to measure chlorine, pH, turbidity (presence of particulates), 
temperature, conductivity, colour, and the presence of bacteria such as e-coli and 
coliform which are indicator species for possible contamination.  
 
Recent testing has shown that while turbidity levels in the system as a whole meet 
the Island Health criteria, the number of days showing turbidity in Holland Lake has 
led staff to recommend testing out a secondary filtration system in addition to the 
recently upgraded chlorination system. 
 
Council is considering the staff recommendation and will discuss the matter further 
in upcoming meetings related to the 2014 budget.
