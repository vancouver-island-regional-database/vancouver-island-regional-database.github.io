Background: Working Together for a Shared Future 
Council members of the Stz’uminus First Nation and t he Town of Ladysmith first signed a 
Community Accord in 2007, in which they pledged to strengthen their relationship and work 
together on matters of common interest. 
 
In 2012, these commitments were renewed under an updated Accord, titled the Naut’sa mawt 
Community Accord (“working together” in the Hul’qumi’num language). Updates to the Accord 
included: 
- a new section on implementing joint initiatives;  
- a commitment to joint education and communications; 
  
- a pact to develop further agreements together; and,  
- language that better reflects the respect and streng thened relationship between the 
two communities.   
 
The purpose of the Accord remains the same: to formally recognize and understand the 
common interests and goals of the two communities so that a strong framework is in place for 
community leaders to pursue, in partnership, initiatives and opportunities that serve greater 
good.

Joint Initiatives: Opportunities to Work Together 
A new section (“Implementation”) in the 2012 Accord emphasized putting ideas into action. A 
smaller working committee was formed to meet quarterly or as needed to coordinate that 
effort, and the communities agreed on the following items for further discussion and possible 
collaboration. 
Item What is this? 
Liquid Waste Management 
Plan 
This detailed plan was prepared by the Town to ensure that collection, 
treatment and discharge of liquid waste (including sewage and 
stormwater) meets or exceeds federal/provincial environmental standards.  
This Plan also helps us plan for the future and includes capacity at the 
Town’s treatment plant for sanitary sewer services for SFN lands. The SFN 
was invited to sit on the liquid waste management plan committee.  The 
SFN Council received a detailed presentation and the Council endorsed the 
Plan.  This endorsement included support for the relocation of a midden, 
resulting in significant savings to the project, a good outcome for both the 
Town and SFN. 
IR 12 (Oyster Bay) and IR 13 
(Kulleet Bay & Shell Beach) 
water/sewer services 
agreement 
These detailed agreements will set out the terms for extending the Town’s 
water and sewer services to SFN lands at the same level of service 
provided within the Town.  
Holland Lake/Stocking Lake 
pipeline agreement 
These lakes provide drinking water for the Town, the Diamond Area, Saltair 
and will provide drinking water for SFN. This pipeline will connect these 
two sources of drinking water and it is proposed to be located on Crown 
land where SFN has an active woodlot. 
Watershed management Landowners, government and service providers are committed to 
protecting the watershed.  The Town and SFN are stakeholders. 
Operational services The Town and SFN are both interested in sharing resources through service 
arrangements, such as building inspection services or water meter reading. 
Boundary extension The proposed boundary extension is for privately owned forest land which 
is adjacent to the Town and traditional lands of interest to the SFN. 
Harbour clean-up This initiative involves many stakeholders with a long-term aim to restore 
the health of Ladysmith Harbour. 
DL 651 partnership This partnership focuses on marina development within the water lot 
adjacent to the Ladysmith Community Marina. 
Uniform bylaws for 
environmental protection 
Both communities care about the environment.  Through this initiative, we 
will fulfill LWMP commitments.  
Funding opportunities We are committed to accessing external funding to support our work. 
Official Community Plan 
consultation 
Municipalities may develop Official Community Plans that set a long-term 
vision for their community, including policies related to planning and land 
use management. Ladysmith’s current OCP was adopted in 2003. Each 
amendment to the Town’s OCP is referred to the SFN for comment.

South Ladysmith Stz’uminus 
First Nation (Crown) land 
development 
The South Ladysmith Industrial Park is located adjacent to the Peerless 
Road Recycling Centre.  Over one-half of the area is Crown land.  The Town 
and SFN are interested in working together to expand the current land 
available for new economic investment. 
Incremental treaty process The SFN and the Town may work together on Crown land development 
within the Town. 
Inclusion of First Nation’s 
heritage/culture 
The first inhabitants of what is now known as Ladysmith were the people 
of the SFN.  The Town is committed to recognizing SFN history and places 
in the Hul’qumi’num language as a part of its interpretive signage program. 
Emergency Preparedness The Cowichan Valley Regional District provides emergency preparedness 
support for both the Town and SFN.  Joint training is available for the staff 
of both organizations. 
Land Use Strategy Land development for community, social and economic development 
purposes is common to both the SFN and the Town.  This initiative 
supports working together to build resilient communities.
