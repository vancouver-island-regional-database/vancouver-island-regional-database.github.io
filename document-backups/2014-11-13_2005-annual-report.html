O
n behalf of the Council of the Town
of Ladysmith, I wish to extend
warm greetings to each resident of
our community. 
The 2005 Annual Municipal Report
carefully accounts for all the numbers and
activities behind another exciting year in the
Town of Ladysmith.  The real story,
however, continues to be the dedication and
commitment of a whole community of
people working together to create a most
beautiful place to call home. 
The year 2005 proved to be another very
rewarding and successful year in our
community. Highlights of the year include:
• Preparation for the implementation of
organic waste curbside collection program
 the first of its kind in British Columbia
• Completion of a new eco-tourism / kayak
storage facility at Transfer Beach
• Completion of the Spray Park at Transfer
Beach
• Launch of water metering and an
extensive public education water
conservation program
• Ongoing work on major infrastructure
upgrades to our water and wastewater
treatment facilities
• Breaking ground on a new RCMP station
(completion scheduled in 2006)
• Planning for and initiating  a
major expansion to our community
recreation centre including a state-of-the-
art municipally operated fitness centre
(now complete)
• Partnering with the local School District
to plan for upgrades to our local playing
fields
• Partnering with the Province of B.C. to
work towards the development of our
waterfront lands
• Holding two successful and well-attended
Town Hall meetings
Amidst all of the activity, we welcomed two
new Council members in November: Scott
Bastian and Donald Fyfe-Wilson. We also
bid farewell to two long-serving Council
members who were deeply devoted to their
community: Wendy Battie and Glenda
Patterson.

Message from the City Manager
W
e are proud to present our
community with the 2005 Annual
Report. This report highlights
results we have achieved over the past year in
finance, operations and work toward our
Strategic Plan. 
The Towns Strategic Plan was completed in
2004 and covers the years 2004-2008. In 2005,
we focused on organizational changes which
will, over time, improve how we deliver our
services. Our objective is to implement the
Strategic Plan; the challenge is to do it with
our existing resources. 
In 2005, we focused on our people and on our
systems. Here are some of the changes we
made. 
• Organizational change: creating a positive
workplace: We moved toward making all
Town of Ladysmith worksites positive
workplaces by acting to retain and attract
the best employees (introducing the ideals
of a value-based organization, providing
employee and Council training, workplace
coaching and wellness programs).
• Information systems: enhancing software,
networks, intranet and electronic filing. 
• Communication with our citizens: issuing
newsletters and reports, holding Town Hall
meetings.
• Construction of capital projects and other
strategic activities: We began construction
of a new police station, expanded the
community centre and established
partnerships with the school district.
A detailed list of activities is included in this
report under the sections 2005 Municipal
Services and Operations Report (pages 14 -16)
and Strategic Plan 2005 - In Review(pages 26
- 33) 
Our actions in the past year have built a
foundation for the future. In implementing new
systems, we support our staff in ways that
enable them to provide better service to our
citizens. 
In the coming year, we will concentrate on
aligning our departmental efforts to support the
corporate strategic focus. Our biggest
challenge is to make these organizational
changes during a period of incredible
community growth and while undertaking a
significant number of capital projects. We will
continue to focus our efforts on the vision and
strategic directions outlined in the Towns
Strategic Plan. 
As City Manager, my responsibility is to
provide leadership in executing the directions
of Council. It takes a team of hard working
people to get this work done. I would like to
take this opportunity to thank our staff for
consistently rising to the challenge they face:
providing quality service that is affordable to
our taxpayers.
We are fortunate to live in Ladysmith and we
take pride in serving in our Town.
Thank you to Council and our citizens for that
opportunity.
Ruth Malli, City Manager

T
he Town of Ladysmith was
incorporated in 1904. The Town is
responsible for the provision of a
wide variety of local government services.
These include:  
• Transportation network (roads, bridges,
street cleaning, sidewalks, etc.)
• Stormwater Management
• Garbage Collection
• Sanitary Sewer Collection System
• Sewage Treatment System
• Water Management
• Parks, Recreation and Cultural Facilities
and Programs
• Fire Protection
• Land Use Planning
• Economic Development
• Building Permits
• Business Licencing
• Domestic Animal Control
• Bylaw Establishment and Enforcement
• Heritage and Revitalization
• Cemetery
• RCMP
Local government services that are not the
responsibility of the Town of Ladysmith
include: 
• School System (Provincial Government
and Local School Board)
• Social and Health Programs (Provincial
Government) 
• Hospital Care System (Provincial
Government)
• Real Property Assessments (B.C.
Assessment) 
• Solid waste disposal (Cowichan Valley
Regional District)
• Employee Pension Plan (Provincial Plan)
• Debt Marketing (Municipal Finance
Authority)
• Library Collection and Distribution
System (Vancouver Island Regional
Library)
The Town of Ladysmith is represented on
the following Regional Boards which
provide a variety of services for the
residents of Ladysmith.
Cowichan Valley Regional District
Cowichan Valley Regional Hospital District
Vancouver Island Regional Library
The Town of Ladysmith obtains services
from the following inter-municipal
organizations.
Municipal Finance 
Authority (MFA)
A provincial organization that provides for
marketing, placement and administration of
municipal debt requirements. This Authority
also operates an investment pool and leasing
on behalf of municipalities.
Municipal Insurance 
Association (MIA)
The Town is a subscribed member of the
Municipal Insurance Association of British
Columbia (The Exchange) as provided by
Section 3.02 of the Insurance Act of the
Province of British Columbia. The main
purpose of the Exchange is to pool the risks
of liability so as to lessen the impact upon
any Subscriber. Under the Reciprocal
Insurance Exchange Agreement the
Municipality is assessed a premium and a
specific deductible for its claims based on
population.

T
he Town’s first strategic direction is
to ensure wise financial management
and to provide fair value to the
taxpayer. The Finance Department will take
the lead role in this direction. The Finance
Department provides budget services,
accounting and financial management. It
supports strategic decision-making by
providing the necessary financial
information.
In the area of finance, the Town is guided by
a philosophy of pay-as-you-go. Strategies
include user pay, increasing the non-taxation
sources of revenue, building reserves,
minimizing tax increases, maximizing
municipal resources, forming partnerships
and performing business case analysis.
Core Services provided by the Finance
Department include:
• billing and collection
• financial planning and budgeting
• financial management and investment
• financial reporting 
• payment for products and services
Wise Financial
Management

projects.
A1.1 Invite Areas G and H to participate in the collection and analysis of service data (this year ).
A1.2 Collect information from other communities (this year ).
A1.3 Develop and implement a strategy to provide for full sub-regional funding of sub-regional
services (this year).
A2.Develop a comprehensive five-year Financial Planning process.
A2.1 Link existing and future plans with the creation of the Citizens Forum (short term).
A2.2 Engage the community in the creation of the five-year plan (this year & short term).
A2.3 Develop and implement a process of business case analysis (this year & short term).
A2.4 Develop and implement a process of project management (this year & short term).
