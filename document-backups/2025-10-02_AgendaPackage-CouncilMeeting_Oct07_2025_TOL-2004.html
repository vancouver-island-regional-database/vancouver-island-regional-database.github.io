INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND: Subject property The subject property is a 2.23-hectare, undeveloped parcel that will be separated from a larger “hooked” parcel in the Hol land Creek Neighbourhood. The property is currently designated Neighborhood Residential under the OCP, zoned Low-Density Residential (R- 3-A) under the Zoning Bylaw and falls within the Multifamily Development Permit Area (DPA 4) and Riparian Development Permit Area (DPA 6). The site is surrounded by greenspace/parkland with suburban development beyond to the north, west and east. A map of the subject property is provided in Attachment A. The OCP and zoning designations for the property were changed in May of 2024 allowing a “density” transfer, whereby permited low-density units could be consolidated into a smaller development footprint. These changes resulted in infrastructure cost savings for the developer and , in exchange, the Town received a large parkland dedication. As a condition of the OCP and zoning changes, the developer agreed to certain covenant conditions allowing infrastructure servicing the project to be phased in alignment with development phases. The developer has since applied for a development permit for the first phase of the development on the “Eastern Parcel”. PROPOSAL: The developer is proposing an 84 -unit multi -family development consisting of 21 fourplexes. The development requires a multi -family development permit and a riparian development permit, the latter of which will be issued separately by staff. The proposed development permit includes 3 variances: 1. a variance to the required bike parking , to reduce the number of require d long- term bicycle parking spaces from 84 to 42, and the number of oversized bicycle parking spaces from 8 to 0. 2. a variance to maximum retaining wall heights allowing a 16m section of retaining wall supporting a small parking area along the northeastern side of the property to exceed the 3-meter maximum by 0.95 meters. 3. A variance reducing the setback from the future Colonia Drive dedication from 6 meters to 4 meters. Page 18 of 101

ANALYSIS: Development Permit and Variances As summarized in Attachment D, the proposed development is consistent with most of the applicable multifamily development permit guidelines. Staff are supportive of the proposed variances to the bicycle parking requirements, retaining wall height and front yard setbacks. In the case of the variances to the bicycle parking requirements, s taff note that the proposal to provide bike lockers to tenants on request meets the intent of the Zoning Bylaw, while preventing a “cluttered” form and character resulting from too many accessory buildings. Development Permit guideline 11(h) specifically allows vehicle or bicycle parking to be reduced where strict compliance with zoning requirements would undermine the character of the neighbourhood. In this case , the unique housing configuration would necessitate a proliferation of separate buildings or bike lockers to meet the zoning requirements. This w ould result in a loss of greenspace and amenity space. Therefore, staff are supportive of the on-demand locker option. In the case of the variance to the retaining wall height requirement, staff note that a minor, isolated height variance is needed to maintain suitable grades for parking lot access. This is consistent with DP guidelines related to site grading and blending the development with existing topography and is therefore supported by staff.
