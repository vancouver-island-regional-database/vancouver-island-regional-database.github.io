MOVIE MAGIC IN 
DOWNTOWN LADYSMITH
Sonic the Hedgehog created movie magic in La-
dysmith during the month of September. The $90 
million Hollywood production starring James 
Marsden, Tika Sumpter and Jim Carrey rolled 
into Ladysmith, creating a big-budget, ﬁ lmmaking 
extravaganza, the likes of which the town had never 
before experienced. 1st Avenue was transformed 
into Green Hills, Montana, complete with restau-
rants, a hat shop, market, sheriff’s station and U.S. 
postal service. There were even exploding cars 
and ﬁ reballs! During day and night scenes, excited 
crowds of movie-lovers gathered to watch, while 
many Ladysmith and area residents were lucky 
enough to act as extras in the ﬁ lm. All the while, 
Ladysmith remained open for business and people 
were encouraged to check out their favourite stores 
and restaurants.
The Town is grateful to Ladysmith business owners, 
employees, patrons and residents for their patience and 
ﬂ exibility. You will notice that the ﬁ lm crew worked hard 
to return everything to its original condition, although 
some shops chose to keep the colours chosen especially 
for the movie shoot. We are all looking forward to watch-
ing the ﬁ nished product late 2019!

imaging cameras thanks to a generous donation from

allow ﬁ reﬁ ghters to judge a building’s hot spots, ﬁ nd 
out where ﬁ res are located, and determine if some-
one is trapped within a burning structure. The cam-
eras can also be used in other lifesaving situations,

from a vehicle following a crash. The T own and Lady-

for the Ladysmith Healthcare Auxiliary’s continued 
support of the health and wellness of our community.

HALLOWEEN SAFETY 
Autumn brings with it falling leaves, cooler 
temperatures and little ghosts and goblins in search 
of candy. With Halloween around the corner, here 
are a few tips for ensuring your children can have fun 
and remain safe while trick or treating:
• Wear a light-coloured or bright 
costume, reﬂ ective tape or arm 
bands to heighten visibility.
• Wear costumes that are properly 
ﬁ tted to reduce tripping hazards.
• Select costumes constructed from 
ﬂ ame-retardant materials.
• Make sure your vision is not restricted. 
Consider wearing make-up instead of masks.
• Create a fun necklace with string and glow 
sticks or reﬂ ective tape to keep kids visible 
to drivers. Or accessorize with a ﬂ ashlight!
• Stay on the sidewalk and never jaywalk.
• Trick or treat in pairs or groups.
• Make sure your kids ask you to check 
their candy before they eat it.
