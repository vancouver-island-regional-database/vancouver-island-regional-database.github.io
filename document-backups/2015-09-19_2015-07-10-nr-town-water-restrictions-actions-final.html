STAGE 3 WATER RESTRICTIONS – WHAT THE TOWN IS DOING 
LADYSMITH, July 10, 2015 – In response to Vancouver Island’s severe drought 
conditions the Town is taking a number of actions to comply with Ladysmith’s Stage 
3 water restrictions.   
Irrigation sprinklers and drip irrigation hoses have been turned off.  Town parks, 
lawns, sports fields, highway boulevards  and medians,  and the cemetery are no t 
being irrigated.  Vegetables, trees and some flower beds will be watered by hand .  An 
irrigation system will be used to water the strawberry plants at Spirit Square  and 
because of the high cost of replacing the greens, the Golf Course will be irrigated. 
The Town will continue growing vegetables, which are donated to the Ladysmith Food 
Bank.  200 s treet trees will be watered twice a week with 20 litre (4 gallon) water 
bags. T he 60 flower baskets from the downtown area have been taken down and 
they are being hand watered with recycled water.  
The fountain in the roundabout at Symonds Street ha s been turned off and will stay 
off as long as water restrictions are in place. 
The Spray Park at Transfer Beach will now be on reduced hours, from 11 :30 a.m. to 
6:30 p.m. daily.   These hours could be cut back further if the reservoir levels dip 
further and the drought continues.  Spray park water will continue to be recycled and 
used for irrigation of the grass at Transfer Beach.  No fresh water will b e used for 
watering. 
Town work vehicles will be washed only when necessary for health or safety reasons, 
and then only when the water can be recycled for use in the Town’s composting 
operations at the Public Works yard.   
“Council and staff take water rest rictions very seriously and we have implemented 
some pretty extreme measures to show citizens we are doing our part,” said Mayor 
Aaron Stone.  “Typically, municipalities and School Districts do get some exemptions 
from following water restrictions because they have to maintain sports fields and 

 
 
other public facilities.  However, we all feel strongly that everything that can be done 
to conserve water, must be done.” 
The Town started using hardy, drought-tolerant plantings several years ago , making 
exceptions only for vegetables and some annuals .  Examples can be seen at the 
Community Services Centre/Spirit Square, the Symonds Street roundabout and Frank 
Jameson Community Centre. 
Ladysmith’s Stage 3 water restrictions are consistent with others in the Cowichan  
Valley Regional District and other communities to the north .  Each municipality is 
responsible for setting its own series of restrictions.   
The long spell of  hot dry weather has led to increased evaporation in the Town’s 
water supply system, as well as higher water demand as residents used more water 
to irrigate before water restrictions were put in place.  However, although  the water 
level in both Stocking and Holland Lakes was down by about 20 per cent at the end 
of June, the Town still has sufficient reserves to provide over 200 days of water at the 
usage levels recorded in June.  The anticipated reduction of water use under Stage 3 
restrictions will extend that reserve. 
The Town regularly posts water use charts and other information about the water 
supply system on its website.  Visit www.ladysmith.ca/our-services/water-
sewer/water to view water data files and a number of related reports. 
Residents are reminded to make sur e their neighbours are aware of the water 
restrictions, and to contact City Hall with any related concerns.
