3. Who can vote? 
You are eligible to vote as a Resident Elector in the Town of Ladysmith if you meet the 
following criteria: 
• A Canadian citizen; 
• 18 years of age or older when you register to vote, or 18 years or older on General 
Voting Day; 
• A resident of BC for at least six months immediately before the day you register to 
vote; 
• A resident of the Town of Ladysmith; AND 
• Not disqualified under the Local Government Act, or any other enactment, or by law 
from voting in a local election. 
Voting rights are granted to Canadian citizens based on residency or property ownership. 
Unfortunately landed immigrants or permanent residents that are not yet Canadian 
citizens are not permitted to vote.  
Proof of identification is not required if you are on the Voter’s List.

4. I own property in Ladysmith but live elsewhere – can I vote? 
Yes, as a Non-Resident Property Elector however, some conditions apply: 
• You are eligible to vote as a Resident Elector in another municipality or regional 
district in BC and not in Ladysmith [i.e. a Canadian citizen, 18 years of age or older, a 
resident of BC for at least six months, a resident of another local government in BC, 
and not disqualified]; 
• You have been a registered owner of real property in the Town of Ladysmith for at 
least 30 days immediately preceding the day of registration;  
• You must provide proof of ownership of the property, listing any other property 
owners on title along with their consent, if applicable; AND 
• You are not disqualified under the Local Government Act, or any other enactment, or 
by law from voting in a local election. 
A person cannot vote on behalf of a corporation, or as a Non-Resident Property Elector, 
based on a property owned wholly or in part by a corporation. If you own the property 
with a corporation, none of the property owners are eligible to vote as a Non-Resident 
Property Elector in relation to the property. 
Regardless of the number of properties owned, a person may only register to vote once as 
a Non-Resident Property Elector. 
To minimize your time and any inconvenience that may arise in the case of multiple owners, 
Non-Resident Property Electors are encouraged to apply ahead of time for a "Non-Resident 
Property Elector Application (Form No.7-2)" by contacting the Chief Election Officer. 
Non-Resident Property Electors must: 
• produce 2 pieces of identification (at least one with a signature) to prove identity; 
• provide proof that they are entitled to register in relation to the property (see list 
below); and 
• provide written consent from the majority of the property owners if there is more 
than one owner of the property. 
 
The following documents are all acceptable proof of ownership: 
• Property Tax Notice 
• Land Title Registration 
• BC Assessment Notice

5. Can someone assist me when I vote? 
If you wish to vote in person, there are several ways we can assist you, listed below.  
Another option is mail ballot voting, discussed under FAQ #2. 
 
ACCESSIBLE 
All Voting Places are wheelchair accessible (City Hall-side door on October 5th and 12th; 
and FJCC on October 15th). 
 
CURBSIDE VOTING 
If you can make it to the Voting Place but are unable to enter because of physical disability 
or impaired mobility please have someone come into the Voting Place and ask for 
"curbside voting".  
 
Election Officials will bring a ballot to you in your vehicle in the parking lot, wait while you 
mark the ballot and, on your behalf, deposit the ballot into the vote counting unit in the 
Voting Place. 
 
MARKING YOUR BALLOT 
You can ask an Election Official, friend or relative to assist if you are unable to mark your 
own ballot. The Local Government Act requires the person assisting an elector to first sign 
a solemn declaration with the Chief Election Officer committing to: preserving the 
secrecy of the ballot, marking the ballot as you instruct and not attempting to influence 
how you vote. 
 
TRANSLATION 
Please bring someone to assist you if you need a translator. The translator will also be 
required to sign a solemn declaration that they are able to make the translation and will 
do so to the best of their ability. 
6. I am a business owner in Ladysmith but live elsewhere - can I vote?
 
It depends - corporations do not have the right to vote however, should your business be 
registered in a personal name vs. a company name you may vote. If that’s the case, rules 
and regulations pertaining to Non-Resident Property Electors would apply in this case.

7. How do I confirm that I live or own property within the Town of Ladysmith? 
Some area residents may have a Ladysmith postal address but are actually located within 
an electoral area outside of the municipal boundaries and will need to go to a different 
Voting Place.  
Here are some ways to determine if you’re within the Town boundaries: 
• Ask who collects your taxes: the Town of Ladysmith, another municipality or the 
Province of BC? 
• Check out the Town’s road index.  If you don’t see your address, you probably do 
not live within Town boundaries.  If you are positive you do live in Town and your 
address does not appear, please call City Hall 250.245.6400 to confirm.  
If you find that you are not within Town boundaries, you are most likely in one of the two 
regional districts that are adjacent to the Town of Ladysmith.  Please visit their websites 
to find out where they are holding their elections: 
• Cowichan Valley Regional District (CVRD): https://www.cvrd.ca/988/Elections-
Referenda  
• Regional District of Nanaimo (RDN): https://www.rdn.bc.ca/elections-an d-
elector-approval 
8. Am I on the list of electors?  
The Town of Ladysmith is utilizing the Provincial Voters List to determine eligible voters. 
If you would like to confirm whether your name appears on this list please contact the 
Chief Election Officer at City Hall during regular office hours at vote@ladysmith.ca. 
The Elections BC website provides you with the opportunity to add your name to the 
Provincial Voter’s List at any time.  While the list is updated on a regular basis, late 
registrations may not make it onto the list that the Town receives for the 2022 Election. 
9. What Identification (ID) is required to vote? 
If you are already on the Provincial Voters List you are not required to show identification.  
A person whose name does not appear on the list is required to show two pieces of 
identification (or sign a solemn declaration) before being issued a ballot by the presiding 
election official.  Any of the following classes of documents will be accepted (note that a 
combined Driver’s Licence and Care Card only count as one document). 
The following classes of documents must be accepted as evidence of identity or residency.

10. Who is eligible to run for office?
 
You may run for local government office if you are a Canadian citizen; are at least 18 years 
old on election day; have lived in BC for at least six months; and have not been disqualified 
from voting in a BC local election. 
You do not have to live in the jurisdiction in which you are running for office. 
The Town of Ladysmith Council includes: one (1) Mayor and six (6) Councillors for a term 
of four years. The 2022-2026 term officially begins when the newly-elected Council 
members take their oaths of office at the Inaugural Meeting of Council to be held Tuesday, 
November 1, 2022. 
Individuals interested in running for office should note that that in addition to regular 
Council meetings, you may be appointed to sit on committees or boards. Prospective 
candidates may wish to speak to a current or past member of Council to discuss expected 
time commitments.

11. Who may not run for office? 
You may not run for local government office if you are a judge of the Provincial Court, 
Supreme Court or Court of Appeal; are an employee or salaried officer of the local 
government (unless you have taken a leave of absence to run for office and agree to resign 
if elected); are under sentence for an indictable offence and are in custody or in prison; 
have been found guilty of an elections offence, such as double voting or buying votes, and 
are prohibited from holding office; or are disqualified for not filing a disclosure statement 
in a previous election or for not making an oath of office or attending meetings.

13. When does the nomination period begin? 
The nomination period begins at 9:00 a.m. on Tuesday, August 30, 2022 and ends at 4:00 
p.m. on Friday, September 9, 2022.  When a candidate is ready t submit their papers, it is 
strongly recommended that an appointment be scheduled with the Chief or Deputy Chief 
Election Officers to go over each of the documents to ensure all the required information 
has been provided. 
Potential candidates are reminded that in order to run as a candidate, you must be 
nominated by two (2) people who are eligible to vote - either as a resident or non-resident 
property elector in the Town of Ladysmith.
 
14. What is the pay for the positions of Mayor and Councillor? 
The Town of Ladysmith’s “Council Remuneration Bylaw 2018, No. 1986”states that in 
2022, the Mayor receives $42,912, and each Councillor receives $16,166.
 
15. What are the Voting Day rules and regulations? 
Here are some of the key rules to follow on Voting Days: 
• No campaigning within 100 metres of the Voting Place, including no displaying signs, 
ads, bumper stickers, posters, buttons, flyers or other material that advocates for or 
against a candidate. 
• No influencing voters within 100 metres of the Voting Place. 
• Only eligible electors are permitted in the Voting Place - and they must leave 
immediately after voting. 'Future voters' (children) are welcome, subject to close adult 
supervision.

• Voting is by secret ballot - no talking about how you or someone else votes inside the 
Voting Place, no displaying your ballot and only one person per voting booth 
• No vote buying or intimidation. 
• No signing false statements or declarations. 
• Turn off all cameras, phones and technology BEFORE entering the Voting Place - it is 
unlawful to reproduce a ballot. 
16. I am a resident of Stz’uminus First Nation – can I vote in Ladysmith’s local election? 
Residents of Stz’uminus First Nation (Oyster Bay Reserve No. 12) are located outside the 
Town’s boundaries, within CVRD Electoral Area H (North Oyster/Diamond).  This means 
they can vote in the election run by the CVRD, not the Town of Ladysmith. 
The CVRD website
 provides information about the voting location (North Oyster 
Elementary School, 13270 Cedar Road, North Oyster). 
For more information about voting from a Reserve, check out the Province's Voters Guide 
for Electors Living on Reserve.
