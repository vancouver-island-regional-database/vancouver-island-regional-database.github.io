  SITE SURVEY AND HEIGHT CALCULATIONS  
 
On the survey drawing (also called a location certiﬁcate) the following information must be provided. 
• Building envelope which means an illustration of the minimum required setbacks from all property lines for 
the Zone that your property is in. Setback information can be found in the Zoning Bylaw. 
• The location of all buildings (existing and proposed) with setbacks from property lines 
• Rights of way, easements, covenant areas, Streamside Protection and Enhancement Area (SPEA) which is 
usually associated with a watercourse on or near the property, if any 
• A note or chart including: 
 
Zone that your property is in (refer to the Zoning Bylaw Map)  
Proposed building height (m)  
Proposed perimeter wall height if the building is in the R-1 Zone.  
Maximum permitted building height (see the Zoning Bylaw)  
Average natural grade (as deﬁned in the Zoning Bylaw)  
Average ﬁnished grade (as deﬁned in the Zoning Bylaw)  
Parcel size (m2)  
Parcel coverage (%) (calculated as per the Zoning Bylaw)  
• Proposed retaining wall height, if any. A building permit is needed if the height is over 1.2 meters (4 feet). 
(Refer to the Zoning Bylaw for height regulations.) 
   Building siting is to be based on a survey prepared by a BC Land Surveyor (BCLS).

  HAZMAT REPORT – SUBSTANTIAL RENOVATION  
   If the building was constructed or renovated prior to 1990, you may need to provide a Hazardous Materials report. Has a 
Hazardous Materials Survey as outlined in WorkSafeBC Guideline 20.112 been completed for this project? Is there a 
presence of any potential asbestos-containing or other hazardous materials? If so, the survey and evidence that the 
disturbed material was removed and disposed of properly must be posted at the worksite entrance so that the Building 
Inspector can inspect the project. 
https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-20-
construction-excavation-and-demolition#SectionNumber:20.112
