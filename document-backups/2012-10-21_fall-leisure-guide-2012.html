Mayor’s Welcome
I recently returned to Ladysmith 
after a short vacation, only to be 
reminded of the many features of 
our town that are often sought, 
or recognized by those who travel 
far afield.  We have beautiful parks 
and trails, a vibrant downtown, our 
heritage is celebrated, a beautiful 
harbour, and countless other 
amenities that make Ladysmith so 
unique and exquisite.  
I also realized that some of our 
greatest assets are those that are not as obvious to see at first glance. 
Our community vision says “Ladysmith is a spirited community 
that values its small town quality of life, where we work together 
as stewards of our heritage, environment, and economy. ”  The key 
to this vision is the people of this community, our shared values, 
how we collaborate, and the many services that support healthy 
individuals in a healthy and vibrant community.  In “Connecting 
the Dots” on page 42, you will read about some milestones and 
achievements being celebrated this year. 
I would also encourage you to learn more about the joint initiatives, 
events, services, and opportunities of Ladysmith Parks, Recreation 
and Culture, a municipal department committed to enhancing 
the health and quality of life of the community through inclusive, 
accessible programs, services, facilities, events, and open spaces.  It 
really is “about the people” , so let’s get to know each other better!  
Mayor Rob Hutchins , Town of Ladysmith

Registration
Full payment is required at time of registration.  Payment is accepted 
by cheque, credit card, debit card, or cash.  All registrations are 
processed on a first-come, first-served basis.  Register early to avoid 
cancellation or disappointment.
4 Easy Ways To Register
1. Drop it off to the Frank Jameson Community Centre, 810 - Sixth  
Avenue, Ladysmith; 
2. Mail it: complete the registration form available online at www.
ladysmith.ca and mail to Box 220, Ladysmith, BC, V9G 1A2;  
3. Fax it or Email it: Complete the registration form available 
online at www.ladysmith.ca and fax it to 250.245.6423 or 
email to prc@ladysmith.ca
4. Phone it in: call 250.245.6424. 
Waiting Lists
If the course you are interested in is full, you may be put on a 
waiting list; however, there is no guarantee that a space will become 
available.  Participants will be contacted only if a space becomes 
available.
Cancellations
Programs are subject to cancellation due to insufficient registration.  
If this occurs, a full refund will be issued.  In the event that a program 
or an individual class is cancelled, every attempt will be made to 
have the class or program rescheduled.
Combined Aquatic Programs
We appreciate your understanding when we are occasionally 
required to reschedule or combine classes, usually due to insufficient 
registrations.  In order to avoid cancelling classes, we may move your 
child to the same level or a combined class being offered at another 
time.
Returned Cheques
A service fee will be levied on all cheques returned “Non Sufficient 
Funds” , “Stop Payment” , or “Funds Not Cleared” .
Refunds
Refunds may be given for withdrawals prior to the start of the 
program unless refund / withdrawal deadline is noted (see 
below).  Prorated refunds may be given once the program begins.  If 
more than half the program has elapsed, no refund or credit will be 
offered.

Age and Height Restrictions
All children under the age of 7 years and/or are less than 1.3 metres tall must be accompanied in the water and within arms reach of a parent or 
other person at least 19 years of age. Ratio of children under 7 to adult must be no greater than 2 children to one adult.

There are 5 family change rooms, one equipped for special needs with a ceiling lift and a changing bed. These rooms have been designed for 
patrons with special needs, parents with small children and those requiring additional privacy.  
Aquatic Wheelchair 
Aquatic wheelchairs are available for those with mobility challenges to enable direct access from the change rooms into the therapy pool. Please

The Whirlpool
The whirlpool is a combination of turbulent air and 40°C (104°F) water pumped into the pool, relieving pain and relaxing muscle spasms,

congestion, in addition to providing overall relaxation.

Therapy/Leisure Pool

Why Water?
Every move you make in water has to be deliberate, resisting the 
continual pressure of the water on your body.  
Water Resistance
Using the resistance of water in exercise is like exercising with 
weights or machines on land but safer. (According to your needs 
and limitations)
Buoyancy
In chest deep water, approximately 90% of your body weight is 
buoyant; therefore you really are bearing only 10% of your weight 
when you exercise. Exercise in the water is much safer and reduces 
stress on your joints and muscles. 
Water Temperature
The water temperature is consistent all year long.  You do not have 
to contend with variable elements such as heat, cold, rain, snow, 
etc.
Cooling Effect
With your continual movement in cool water your body is kept from 
overheating.  Drinking water while exercising in water is just as 
important as when exercising on dry land.
Aquatic Exercise Benefits: 
Fitness
Cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength and endurance, 
flexibility, coordination, agility, power and balance are fitness 
components achievable to maintain and improve any fitness level.
Therapeutic
Exercise in water safely enables and assists you in the recovery from 
injury, surgery and illness.  It also helps to combat the aging process 
and has amazing mental therapeutic value.
Stress Relief
The soothing comfort of the water aids in alleviating mental and 
emotional stresses.
Social
Speaks for itself. Social connections  have proven health benefits.
Comfort
The cooling and massaging effects of exercise in the water make it a 
great place to work out without fatigue.
Fun
The aquatic environment and facility atmosphere make it a great 
place to laugh and have fun while doing something great for your 
mind and body.
There are many aquatic fitness aids to help maximize the effect of 
your exercise, including removable stairs and an aquatic lift into the 
main pool, aquatic wheelchairs to help those in need to navigate 
the ramp into the therapy teach pool. There are also massaging jets 
available in the therapy pool.
For your comfort and enjoyment a sauna and hot tub are also 
available.  Please refer to the schedule for all program choices.
H20 – The perfect medium to optimize physical and mental fitness.   With many options available 
the water is the perfect place to help achieve, maintain, and increase fitness levels.  As simple as wa-
ter walking to lap swimming we have something to satisfy the needs of every individual and their 
unique needs.

Aquatic Leadership & First Aid
Bronze Medallion (13 yrs & up)
This program teaches an understanding of the lifesaving 
principles embodied in the four components of water rescue 
education - judgement, knowledge, skill & fitness. Rescuers 
learn both tows and carries, & release methods in preparation for 
challenging rescues of increased risk. Lifesavers learn to respond 
to increasingly complex situations involving consciences and 
unconscious victims with an obstructed airway. Lifesavers also 
develop stroke efficiency & endurance in a timed swim.  This 
program is a prerequisite for Bronze Cross.

This course is designed for lifesavers who want the challenge

6 & up. This level provides an introduction 
to front crawl as well as the foundation 
for making wise choices on where & 
when to swim. Diving is introduced & children will work on floats

Red Cross Level 4 
6 & up. Front crawl, back glide & shoulder roll for back crawl are 
further developed. Children work on kneeling dives, surface support 
& developing a greater sense of self-safety by understanding their 
own limits. Endurance is built through a 25-meter swim.
Red Cross Level 5 
Back crawl is introduced, along with sculling skills & whip kick on 
back. Children try stride dives & receive an introduction to safe 
boating skills. Endurance is developed through dolphin kick & a 
50-metre swim.
Red Cross Level 6 
Front & back crawl continue to be refined as the elementary 
backstroke is newly introduced.  Children are also introduced to 
safety on ice, elementary rescue of others with throwing assists, 
treading water & front dives. Endurance is built through a 75-metre 
swim.
Red Cross Level 7 
Continues to build skills & endurance for front crawl, back crawl & 
elementary back stroke.  Introduction to  whip kick on the front. 
Children learn about airway & breathing obstructions, participate 
in timed treading water for increasing endurance as well as an 
endurance swim of 150-metres. 
Red Cross Level 8 
This level provides an introduction to breaststroke, foot first 
surface dives, & rescue entries. Children learn about the dangers of 
open water, hypothermia, & the performance of rescue breathing 
on children & adults.  Endurance is built through dolphin kick & 
300-metre swim. 
Red Cross Level 9 
Front & back crawl, elementary back, side & breast strokes continue 
to be refined. Dives & wise choices, peer influences & self rescue from 
ice are covered. Endurance is built through a 400-metre swim.
Red Cross Level 10 
This level covers refinement of all strokes, fitness & sun safety, rescue 
of others from ice, & head & foot first surface dives. Endurance is built 
through a 500-metre swim.

Connecting the Dots for a Healthy Community
So…are you healthy?  Really healthy?  Often when we hear the word “health” , we think of medical care, and the systems that fix people once they become 
sick.  A better understanding of the word might be the World Health Organization definition, “a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being 
and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. ”  The Public Health Agency of Canada lists the “Key Determinants of Health” , in which “Health Services” 
is tenth on a list of twelve factors influencing the health of individuals and their communities.  Your well-being is shaped more by income and social status, 
social support networks, working conditions, social and physical environments, and personal health practices and coping skills.  These factors are found 
not in hospitals, but in communities.
The essence of community was recently seen in the establishment of the Ladysmith Community Services Centre and Spirit Square, and there are many 
other examples of the links forged among neighbours and organizations which have helped to create a strong network supporting healthy individuals in 
our healthy community.  There is the Community Garden and its connection to the Food Bank; the Arts Council and its community art classes; LaFF and 
its many local resource partners; programs such as “Steady Feet” cosponsored by the Vancouver Island Health Authority and Ladysmith Parks Recreation 
& Culture.  This year marks a number of milestones for our Town and our community organizations: the Ladysmith Department of Parks, Recreation and 
Culture celebrated its 30 year anniversary in May, the Ladysmith Resources Centre Association 20 years, and the Boys and Girls Club 40 years!  Perhaps most 
significantly, the Town of Ladysmith and the Stz’uminus First Nation recently observed 5 years since the first signing of the 2007 Community Accord with a 
renewal of that accord, entitled “Naut’sa Mawt” , or “working together” .
The Town of Ladysmith also participates in the Cowichan Communities Health Network (CCHN), whose vision is that all citizens of the Cowichan communities 
enjoy good health.  The CCHN, chaired by Ladysmith Mayor and CVRD Chair Rob Hutchins, acts as a catalyst for action on the 
determinants of health, through collaboration, partnerships, and shared responsibilities.  Look for your chance to connect 
with the CCHN and the Ladysmith Community Health Advisory Committee at the annual Fall Health and Community Services 
Fair in October.  And as if we needed reminding, new findings published in the British medical journal “Lancet” leading up 
to this summer’s Olympics and Paralympics in London reaffirm the importance of physical activity for maintaining good 
health.  You have seen countless ways to get 
active throughout this guide, so take advantage 
of the many opportunities available to you, and 
“just do it!”
I speak for our Council and community as we look forward to building 
on our partnership with the Town of Ladysmith, and to future 
partnerships with other communities and organizations, that’s what 
it’s all about. Chief and Council of Stz’uminus First Nation are in full 
support of this relationship, building on the Accord to create future 
opportunities, supporting progress and moving forward.  
Chief Councillor John Elliott, Stz’uminus First Nation
Reflecting on the community of Ladysmith and recent milestones 
celebrations – the Ladysmith Resources Centre Association 20th 
anniversary and the 30th anniversary of Ladysmith Parks, Recreation 
& Culture, I am even more inspired by the people of Ladysmith.  
People here seem to have goals similar to those of both the LRCA 
and the LPRC, to support and sustain a healthy community.  And 
by “people” I really mean individuals, service clubs, the Health Care 
Auxiliary, youth groups, merchants and the Town of Ladysmith itself, 
all of whom actively support this goal through financial donations, 
giving of time, expertise, sharing resources, and cooperation to create 
a caring, healthy, successful community.  Ladysmith is a big-hearted 
town where a lot of dreams come true and I’m so proud to live here.
Fran Willgress, President, Ladysmith Resources Centre Association 
Board of Directors
The theme of “Connecting the Dots” gives me the opportunity to reflect on 
the history of our work in Ladysmith.  The Boys and Girls Clubs of Central 
Vancouver Island celebrates our 40th anniversary this year, just as the 
Ladysmith Resources Centre Society - LRCA (20th) and Ladysmith Parks and

for the health of children and youth in Ladysmith.  If there is one thing we

success we dream of without working together.
Kevin Douville, President, Boys & Girls Clubs of Central Vancouver Island
