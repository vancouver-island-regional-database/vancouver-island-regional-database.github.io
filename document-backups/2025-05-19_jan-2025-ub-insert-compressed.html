2025 By-Election Declared for 
March 1
The 2025 by-election for the mayoral seat on Ladysmith 
Town Council has officially been declared by the Chief 
Election Officer for Saturday, March 1.
Voting will take place on election
 day between the hours of 
8:00 am
 and 8:00 pm  at the Frank Jameson Community 
Centre. Advance  voting opportunities are available to 
qualified electors on Wednesday, February 19 and 
Wednesday, February 26 from 8:00 am to 8:00 pm at City 
Hall.
For those interested in running,
 nomination packages are 
available on
 the Town's
 website or
 at City  Hall, 410 
Esplanade. Completed
 packages must be returned to City 
Hall between January 14 and January 24. Early or late 
submissions will not be accepted.
Council officially appointed Donna Smith as Chief 
Election Officer to oversee 
the upcoming by-election 
during a Special Meeting of Council on Wednesday, 
December 11. The Chief Election Officer is tasked with 
overseeing all aspects of the by-election, in accordance 
with provincial legislation.
T
he by-election was triggered after Mayor Aaron Stone 
stepped down from his role as mayor in September. 
“The Town of Ladysmith would like to once again express 
its gratitude to former Mayor Aaron Stone for his 
unwavering dedication and exceptional service 
to our co mmunity throughout his tenure,” says 
Acting Mayor McKay.

What Happens When it Snows in 
Ladysmith
In the event of a snowfall, the first priority for the 
plows is to clear access to public buildings such as
t
he Community Health Centre, RCMP detachment, 
Ambulance Station and Fire/Rescue Station.
We then clear and maintain all main roads on a 24-hour 
basis as the snow continues to fall. 
Our primary concern is keeping the main roadways—
1st 
 Avenue, 4 th Avenue, Do gwood Drive, Davis 
Road and Che mainus Road—open to traffic.
W
eather permitting, we then clear snow from the main 
hills throughout the Town, focusing on widening the 
r
oads we have already cleared to make room for traffic. 
L
ocal neighbourhood roads will be cleared after the 
main thoroughfares (listed above) are completed.
Through roads will be cleared first, and then cul-de-sacs. 
Did you know it can take about 48 hours to clear all 60 
kilometres of roads in Ladysmith? Remember that it is 
your responsibility to shovel the sidewalks bordering 
your property by 10:00 am after a snowfall. This helps 
to
 ensure the safety of your neighbours and other 
pedestrians.

Have you ever wanted to show off your artwork in a way that an entire community could see it? 
Well, now is your chance.  The Town is inviting artists of all ages from Ladysmith, Stz’uminus 
and the surrounding area to submit designs for the 2025 Community Banner Program. 
Designs must represent Ladysmith’s small-town charm, community life and diverse cultural 
heritage, while highlighting the Community Banner Program’s theme.  This year’s theme, “Coastal 
Living: Growing Our Future Together,” celebrates Ladysmith’s stunning seaside, which supports an 
abundance of life and serves as a place of growth and healing for all our residents.
“
The Community Banner Program has been a source of joy and pride in Ladysmith since its inception. 
O
ver 150 banners are displayed on light standards throughout our historic downtown each year, bringing 
vibrancy 
and strengthening our sense of community during the high season for tourism and local 
events,” says Acting Mayor McKay.
The winning designs will be displayed along 1 st Avenue. An honorarium of $1,000 will be awarded to 
the artist whose designs are chosen by the Selection Committee. Artists must submit a completed 
entry form that includes their designs to bid@ladysmith.ca before 4:00 pm on Frida y, January 31, 2025.
