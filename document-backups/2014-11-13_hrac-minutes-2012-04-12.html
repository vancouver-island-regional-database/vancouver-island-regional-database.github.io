b. Heritage Façade Grant Program – Building Survey
 
P. Blumel and B. McLaurin presented their building survey inventory to the 
Commission. The purpose of the buildi ng survey was to determine the 
general condition of heritage building facades, including an assessment of 
the building’s paint. The facades were assessed on a scale from excellent to 
poor for both paint finish and visible condition. The inventory was divided into 
three sections: Phase 1, for buildings included in the heritage building 
brochure (itself based on the 1990 Ladysmith Inventory), Phase 2, for 
additional buildings over 60 years old that may be added to the list of 
heritage buildings, and Phase 3, for all historic buildings and Miner’s cottages 
located throughout Ladysmith. The phases are intended to suggest a base for 
allocation of funding.

6. HERITAGE PROJECT UPDATES 
a. Heritage Building Plaques - Wigwam
 
A. McDermid worked with the LDHS Archives to research the history of the 
Wigwam Restaurant to amend the Nicholson Block heritage plaque. An 
advertisement for the Wigwam was foun d in a 1925 newspaper, the earliest 
record to be located. The Wigwam restaurant and date has been added to the 
plaque. A sub-committee will present the plaque to the owner of the Wigwam 
for review. 
 
b. Canadian Register of Historic Places 
 
It had been noted by P. Blumel that St . John’s Anglican Church and Aggie Hall 
weren’t included on Canada’s Regist er of Historic Places, despite the 
application and provision of a Statemen t of Significance for each building. 
Staff reported that correspondence wi th the Registrar of the BC Heritage 
Branch indicated that the records may have been misplaced due to the 
volume of records submitted in 2010. The two properties will hopefully be 
added to the Canadian Register within a month. 
 
c. Transfer Beach Pictorial

7. NEW BUSINESS   
a. Public Notice Board at Traveller’s Hotel
 
B. McLaurin noted that the windows on the Traveller’s Hotel are frequently 
covered in posters, adding to the a ppearance of a vacant and dilapidated 
building. The Commission discussed the need for additional community 
notice boards in the Town to replace the use of buildings as notice boards. T. 
Hutchinson noted that Nanaimo had new circular notice boards which add to 
the aesthetic appeal of the street. A. McDermid noted that the boards could 
also provide paid advertising space to contribute to the cost of the boards. 
 
It was moved, seconded and carried that the Heritage Advisory Revitalization Commission 
recommend to Council that the To wn consider the installation of a public notice board on 
the west side of First Avenue to limit the notices posted on the Traveller’s Hotel.
