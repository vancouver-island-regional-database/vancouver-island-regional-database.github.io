ATTACHMENT D DPA 4 Guidelines Staff Comments 1. Building Design Scale/neighbourhood identity  The architectural theme and massing of the proposed buildings is consistent with the suburban residential character in the surrounding neighbourhood. Exterior design aesthetics, materials, and colours  Building facades are articulated using varied materials, finishes, balconies and rooflines. Three exterior wall material schemes are proposed for the buildings, including board and batten siding, cement lap siding, and fiber cement sidewall shingles. Design aesthetic of neo -traditional, Pacific Northwest, or eco -responsive themes  Building designs incorporate neo-traditional and contemporary themes. Resembles single unit dwellings  The proposed buildings resemble the existing massing and style of single detached homes in the neighbourhood along South Colonia Drive. Variation in building designs (building designs should not be repeated on the same street, nor located directly across the street from each other)  The u se of six different fourplex designs and 3 different colour palettes provides variation in building designs massing. 2. Building Siting and Massing Height and massing variation and roofline treatments between buildings  The topography of the land and proposed grades will provide for variation in height, and the six fourplex building types will offer changes in massing and some height variation. Building design stepped with topography, nestled into hillside, with cuts an d fills blending with the topography  Buildings incorporate slopping topography into the design with walkout basements and similar features. The use of retaining walls throughout the site is minimal. Passive heating/cooling from landscaping  Deciduous tree plantings are proposed along different elevations of the buildings , including the southeast elevations and southwest elevations. This will allow for light penetration in the winter months and shading during the summer season. 3. Building Frontages Building frontages articulated and visually broken-up  Building frontages are articulated by slight stepping and recessing of the wall facings.  Awnings, balconies and decks provide additional articulation. Facades modulated through design methods  Most buildings have some modulation by stepping and recessing the building faces and in the roof lines and their orientations. Building Type C does not have protrusions and recessions; however, the decks at the rear of the buildings provide some modulation and the timber posts on the front elevation add some minor dimension to the front building elevation. Other building types also include timber posts and balconies/decks to add modulation. Orientation towards street  Most of the buildings are oriented towards the street (Colonia Drive). While some buildings are oriented towards off-street parking areas, there is a continuous building line along Colonia Drive.

Streetscapes and building facades are not dominated by garages.  No garages are proposed. Buildings on street corners should orient frontages towards both streets. N/A 4. Roof Form Sloping roof forms encouraged. • All principal building types have sloping roofs. Roofline modulation to provide visual interest • Rooflines are modulated through ridge variations that are both parallel and perpendicular to the street. Gables are accented with sidewall shingles. 5. Windows & Doors Building entrances are clearly defined. Townhouses have separate, street- oriented entrances and recessed doorways  Buildings fronting Colonia Drive have doors and windows oriented towards the street. Windows are architecturally compatible with building  Windows are complimentary to the design of the buildings and are accentuated with dark trim to contrast buildings colours. 6. Signs, Canopies & Lighting Compatible signage  No signage is proposed. Canopies or other building projections should provide weather protection  Most building entrances are recessed and /or covered with projecting balconies or roof overhangs. Adequate lighting to illuminate sidewalk areas adjacent to buildings  Street lighting on Colonia Drive will be built in accordance with municipal standards.  The proposed lighting plan utilizes full cutoff LED lighting and bollard luminaires to direct downward and towards high traffic areas. Light fixtures should be concealed Exterior lighting should follow dark sky principles 7. Liveability Visual privacy of interior living maintained  Privacy has been considered in the site plan and interior layouts  Landscaping also provides some additional privacy along the buildings fronting Colonia Drive  Orientation of units and landscaping elements enhance privacy of dwellings. Building and site de sign to mitigate noise impacts of roads and highways  The site will be bisected by Colonia Drive which will not experience highway traffic volumes.  The s ite is a substantial distance from other roads and neighbourhoods and separated by parkland and wooded areas. Private outdoor living spaces should be provided for each dwelling unit  All buildings are designed so each dwelling unit has a small outdoor garden patio or balcony.  Rain gardens areas will include walking paths and picnic benches 8. Materials & Colours Building materials should be durable and high quality and cohesive with the neighbourhood. • Six fourplex designs with subtle colour variations are proposed. Exterior finishes include fiber cement lap horizontal siding, fib er cement board and batten, fibe r cement sidewall shingles, and timber posts which are of high quality and durability. 9. Mechanical, Electrical & Security Equipment

Mechanical equipment should be strategically located and screened.  Small thru-wall air conditioners will be used to meet Building Code requirements for cooling. No large exterior HVAC equipment is proposed. 10. Accessibility & Connectivity Building and sites should be designed to be accessible to all users.  Buildings will meet or exceed building code requirements for accessibility.  Not all units are accessible (i.e. some are walkups). However, covenant conditions require some units to be fully accessible (see site plan), which exceeds building code requirements. Public walkways and entrances should provide a seamless, functional, and safe pedestrian network.  Units are provided with pathways connecting buildings to the street and parking areas. 11. Vehicle & Bicycle Parking Vehicle parking should be located to the rear or side of buildings. Street fronting parking entries, carports, or garage doors are discouraged.  Parking areas are situated behind or beside buildings. Shared vehicle access and shared surface parking areas is encouraged.  Buildings are serviced by shared parking areas. Surface parking areas should delineate driving, parking, and pedestrian area though the use of materials, colours, and patterns. Parking areas should be visually enhanced with landscaping.  Pedestrian pathways connect parking to dwellings and landscaping is provided around parking areas. Off-street parking spaces shall be equipped with one set of electric vehicle charging equipment per 20 spaces.  Electric vehicle charging is provided for 4 parking spaces. Bicycle parking should be provided for every building.  Long-term and short-term bicycle parking is provided. The DP includes a variance to long -term and oversize bike parking requirements, with condition for “on -demand” bike lockers. Given the buildings do not include garages, this is expected to provide a suitable balance between bike parking needs and landscape aesthetics—i.e. the number of detached accessory buildings will be minimized. Parking areas, driveways and walkways should have adequate areas for snow storage and drainage.  Large, landscaped areas adjacent to parking areas and sidewalks provide sufficient space for snow storage. 12. Landscape Site planning should be guided by the preservation of existing trees, shrubs, groundcover, and other natural features.  Trees and vegetation were removed from the site by the previous owner. Disturbed natural areas should be restored to replicate the natural setting.  Disturbed areas are to be replanted in accordance with the landscape plan. Front yard not used for access should be landscaped.  All front yards along Colonia Drive will be landscaped. Only lawn should not be supported.  Proposed lawn areas are enhanced with landscaped ground cover, shrubs, and trees. Retaining walls should be terraced or stepped and plant material incorporated into the wall design.  Landscaping is incorporated into retaining wall designs to reduce visual impact of wall heights.

Concrete retaining walls should include textured concrete on the face of the wall. Untreated large concrete and concrete block walls are not supported.  Retaining walls are small , limited to specific areas and are screened by landscaping or wooded areas. Design and materials used in fences and retaining walls should complement the building design and neighbourhood character.  Split rail fencing will be used and retaining walls are located along wooded and landscaped areas. Surface parking and storage areas should be screened.  Surface parking areas are screened by the development’s layout and landscaping. Multi-purpose landscapes, preferably communal garden areas, should be integrated into the design.  Large landscaped common areas are provided. Landscaped roofs are encouraged.  N/A Landscape groundcover plants should be used rather than extensive mulch or gravel. Use of artificial turf should not be supported.  Graveled areas are minimal. Extensive plantings have been provided throughout the site. Use native, drought tolerant plants. Landscaping that does not require permanent irrigation is encouraged.  The landscape plan incorporates some native, fire resistant and adaptive, drought tolerant plant species.  There is a DP condition requiring high -efficiency irrigation system. Landscape buffers should be provided as per Zoning Bylaw.  No landscape buffer is required, as the property does not abut other residential properties (vegetative buffers alrea dy exist through Natural tree cover). Adequate monetary security may be required to ensure that landscaping will be completed and established.  The DP will require security All landscaping should conform to BC Landscape Standard  A landscape plan for the site has been prepared by a Landscape Architect and conforms to BC Landscape Standards. Onsite monitoring should be undertaken by a landscape professional during installation.  A report from a Landscape professional will be required prior to the release of the required landscape security. 13.
