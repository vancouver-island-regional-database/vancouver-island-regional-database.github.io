Welcome to Ladysmith, British Columbia, Canada, where exceptional amenities, world-class municipal infrastructure, scenic trails, historic downtown charm, and the beauty of Transfer Beach come together to create unparalleled charm along the 49th Parallel. Located on spectacular Vancouver Island, just 88 kilometres (55 miles) north of Victoria and 23 kilometres (14 miles) south of Nanaimo, Ladysmith is a vibrant and growing seaside community (an estimated population of 9,787 in 2025) that proudly maintains its small-town character and friendly community atmosphere. Residents here take tremendous pride in preserving and celebrating the Town’s industrial heritage, while also respectfully acknowledging the more than 5,000 years of history of the land’s first residents, the Stz’uminus First Nation. Whether it’s our vibrant downtown, breathtaking natural surroundings, beloved Transfer Beach, or countless events and festivals —including art shows and the Island-famous Festival of Lights—Ladysmith offers year-round experiences for everyone to enjoy. As a recognized municipal leader, the Town of Ladysmith is committed to setting the standard for progressive, sustainable community development. OUR COMMUNITY 2025 Annual Report 4

On behalf of Ladysmith Town Council, I’m thrilled to present the 2025 Annual Report, highlighting our strategic priorities, accomplishments, and financial outcomes for the year. This year was marked by both successes and significant external challenges, including the downloading of 9-1-1 dispatch costs to our community, threats to Canadian sovereignty, and the implementation of U.S. tariffs. Despite these impacts, we made meaningful progress, moving Ladysmith forward in a positive direction for all residents. Among the year’s most significant projects was the decommissioning of the Colonia and Mackie dams, which restored more than one kilometre of Holland Creek to its natural state, allowing fish to pass freely. Some of the funding for this project was provided through a $2.75-million grant from the Community Emergency Preparedness Fund, administered by the Union of British Columbia Municipalities on behalf of the Ministry of Emergency Management and Climate Readiness. The Agricultural Field stormwater drainage project was also completed on time and on budget. Other notable achievements in 2025 include the replacement of the roof at the Frank Jameson Community Centre (FJCC), the installation of new fitness equipment at FJCC, and the start of Phase 2 of the Forrest Field improvements—including a new pickleball court, bike park, and outdoor fitness circuit. Although not fully complete in 2025, significant progress was made at the Machine Shop, a key part of the Arts and Heritage Hub. As a cornerstone of Ladysmith’s waterfront revitalization, it is set to open to the public in 2026. The Town also advanced accessibility initiatives. The first-ever draft Accessibility Plan was brought to Council, and several improvements were made in the community, including a new ramp at the Ladysmith Museum and an upgraded Rotary Memorial Peace Garden & Cenotaph, making the area wider and more accessible. Each year, Council appoints community members to serve on several Advisory Committees, providing residents with an opportunity to contribute their knowledge, perspectives and lived experiences to local decision-making. Advisory Committees focus on specific areas such as planning, parks and recreation, accessibility and other key community priorities. While the committees do not make final decisions, they provide advice and recommendations to Council on matters that affect the community, helping inform Council’s decisions. In addition to advisory committees, Council also meets as the Committee of the Whole (CoW) on a bimonthly basis. The Committee of the Whole includes all members of Ladysmith Council and provides an opportunity for early discussion of proposed policies, services, and other matters. Significant progress was made in 2025 to advance the Waterfront Area Plan, highlighted by major improvements to the Machine Shop building. Located at the heart of the Arts and Heritage Hub, the Machine Shop is a cornerstone of Ladysmith’s waterfront revitalization and a key component of the community-driven vision developed by residents of Ladysmith and Stz’uminus First Nation. Throughout 2025, the exterior was substantially upgraded with the installation of new doors, a concrete accessible deck, and fully restored windows. A fresh coat of paint has helped transform the once- dormant structure, bringing renewed life to this prominent waterfront space. Inside, new heating and cooling systems were installed to improve energy efficiency, along with major electrical upgrades. Drywall was completed and new rooms were constructed to support the building’s future use. The Town previously secured a $3.6-million grant to support the project and once completed, the Machine Shop will feature two artists studios, an artist teaching space, a meeting room, an indoor display space and an additional 4,600 sq.ft of available space for the community.
