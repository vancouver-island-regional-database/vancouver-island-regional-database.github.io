BACKGROUND 
Couverdon Real Estate (Couverdon) is examining the future land use potential for its lands through a 
comprehensive inventory, analysis, planning, and design process. The Town of Ladysmith is considering 
annexation of the Couverdon lands to facilitate future expansion of the municipa lity. We understand that 
Couverdon is providing the Town of Ladysmith with  a general land use concept to support the proposed 
annexation. Pottinger Gaherty Environmental Consultants Ltd. (PGL) has been retained by Couverdon to provide 
advice on environmentally responsible development strategies to the planning team, Sasaki Associates (Sasaki). 
This memorandum describes our approach, summarizes our work to date, and outlines future environmental work 
for the Couverdon lands.  
Environmental Planning Process 
Environmental planning requires balancing ecological cons iderations with community development and economic 
demands. For the Couverdon lands, environmental values and goals will be integrated into each planning phase 
to help ensure that environmental ri sks are identified and that impact av oidance or mitigation strategies are 
developed and implemented.  
The project is currently at an early stage in the proces s where feasible development areas are being determined 
with input from a variety of sources including environmental specialists. Typically, the first phase of environmental 
planning includes the review of existing data for the site and development of a preliminary understanding of the 
probable sensitive areas. A preliminary reconnaissance survey of the site by field biologists is helpful to interpret 
the existing data and develop advice for preliminary planning.  
PGL has been involved from the outset of the environment al planning process for the lands and participated with 
Sasaki and Couverdon at the Ladysmit h Community Open House in July 2011. Since then we have continued to 
work to identify and qualify overview biophysical constraints on the property so that they may be considered in the 
early stages of planning. Our main focus has been to id entify preliminary environmental sensitivities on the 
Couverdon lands for Sasaki to consider in the development of an early conceptual land use plan. Further work will 
be done to assemble field data so that these areas c an be refined, and environmental planning objectives can be 
defined for different areas based on their level of sensitivity.

PRELIMINARY ENVIRONMENTAL SENSITIVITIES 
PGL has completed a preliminary environmental sensitiv ities mapping exercise for the Couverdon lands. A 
sensitivities map is a tool to spatially  identify the relative environmental valu es in a study area for the purpose of 
minimizing impacts through avoidance of valuable area s at an early planning stage. The planning work will use 
this as a guidance tool for avoiding sensitive areas where possible. It is important to recognize that the map is at a 
coarse level of resolution, and so the boundaries are not exact and will require refinement when the design and 
approvals process requires a higher level of detail. As such, the mitigation of environmental impacts is best 
achieved with an ongoing interactive planning process that is aligned with the steps of the development approvals 
process. There are various environmental assessment tool s that can be used in a planning process such as this, 
and we have identified the key ones below under 'Next Steps'. 
Data Sources 
A number of data sources were used to identify the pr eliminary environmental sensit ivities on the Couverdon 
lands including: 
 Terrestrial ecosystem mapping, forest  cover data, and watercourse mapping; 
 Sensitive Ecosystems Inventory of East Vancouver Island and Gulf Islands (2004); 
 Orthophotography and topographic data (LIDAR); 
 Government databases for fish and wildlife presence, including species at risk; and  
 Field reconnaissance by PGL biologists to: 
o Complete preliminary identification of watercourses and wetlands; 
o Complete preliminary identification of riparian setb acks as per Riparian Areas Regulation (RAR) methods; 
o Ground truth areas of particular sensitivity; and 
o Identify opportunities for ecological en hancement as part of the project. 
Methodology 
Any method that aims to differentiate and characterize t he relative ecological sensitivities in an area must make 
decisions about the priorities for protection. For this  study area, our methodology  to develop a preliminary 
environmental sensitivities map aims to achieve the following objectives: 
A.  Aquatic Ecosystems 
 Maximize protection of wetlands and watercourses (hydrological and ecological functions) and apply 
habitat enhancement measures where most needed. 
 Protect and/or enhance riparian buffers according to the RAR, and seek opportunities to protect riparian 
areas in excess of RAR setbacks where most appropriate. 
B.  Terrestrial Ecosystems 
 Protect and/or enhance rare or sensitive ecosystems , older forests, key wildlife habitat types, and wildlife 
corridors. 
C.  Species at Risk 
 Protect and/or enhance the populations and cr itical habitats of species at risk. 
D.  Biodiversity 
 Protect areas of high biodiversity.

With these objectives in mind, the following steps were taken to develop an environmental sensitivities map: 
1. The data sources were examined to identify their reliab ility (level of rigour, source s of error, etc.). Where 
appropriate, data was updated using fi eld observations and/or desktop methods (i.e., LIDAR contours were 
used to refine hydrology mapping and orthophotography comparison used to refine forest cover data).  
2. Preliminary riparian setbacks were calculated from field measurements on applicable watercourses using 
the RAR methodology. The setbacks are considered preliminary riparian areas for initial planning guidance, 
and will be defined further as needed at a future step in  the development planning process. Riparian areas 
are a priority for conservation so that development will comply with the RAR.  
3. An ecosystem ranking methodology was developed and applied to the Couverdon lands based on the 
objectives listed above. The ecosystem attributes co nsidered in the methodology included forest age 
(i.e., structural stage), tree species diversity, and rarity of the ecosystems present. Greater value was 
assigned to older forest, more diverse forest stands, and provincially rare ecological communities.  
4. The sensitivity map was developed as an aggregation of: (a) the watercourse/wetlands and their riparian 
setbacks; (b) the ecosystem ranks; and (c) a predi ction of key wildlife habitats based on an understanding 
of the important species in the area and their likely habitat uses.  
Sensitivity Mapping Results 
The preliminary environmental sensitivity map is presen ted in Figure 1. The boundaries and attributes of the 
polygons correspond to the level of detail in the original dat a, and are subject to refinement through the analysis 
of additional survey data later in the ap provals process. The map is at a scal e that is typical for conceptual land 
use plans, while recognizing that some discrepancies may need closer examination at a higher level of resolution. 
PGL developed a scale to rank the preliminary environmental  sensitivities. The three ranks are shown in Figure 1 
and are defined as: 
Dark Green 
 Provides a high level of environmental value. 
 Includes wetlands and watercourses which provide fish  habitat (or provide nutrients to fish habitat).  
 Includes riparian areas defined by preliminary RAR assessments.  
 Is a top priority for conservation. 
Light Green 
 Provides a moderate level of environmental value.  
 Includes wetlands and watercourses that do not contribute directly to fish habitat and their riparian areas, but 
likely provide habitat to other wildlife. 
 Includes buffers to the RAR riparian areas, as th ese areas are considered a priority for restoration.  
 Includes other ecosystems that have value based on forest age, diversity, and rarity.  
 Preserving as much of these areas as possible is go od for the environment and would help the project exceed 
standard environmental objectives. 
No Colour 
 Areas with no colour have a lower level of environmental value.  
 The development footprint should focus on these areas. 
 Standard best practices in environmentally  responsible development should be applied. 
 This area is not devoid of environmental value, but it is likely that any key values can be preserved through 
good planning and standard mitigation measures that will be identified as part of a more detailed planning 
stage.

FIELD DATA COLLECTION 
Following the creation of the preliminary sensitivity map, various surveys were conducted in spring 2012 to collect 
field observations and data for use in the next stage of environmental planning. These surveys included: 
 Spring vegetation surveys to verify current data with special focus on rare ecosystems and habitat for rare 
plant species; and 
 Wildlife surveys with focus on species of conservation concern and targeted detection of diurnal raptors, owls, 
herons, songbirds, and amphibians. 
Along with the desktop work described above, the field dat a will provide a solid baseline understanding of the 
property to guide future planning and mitigation of potential impacts. 
NEXT STEPS 
The Couverdon development is in t he early stages of the design and envir onmental planning process. Ongoing 
environmental work will provide greater  detail about biophysical aspects of the property to guide future design 
concepts. The next step for PGL will be to compile and analyze the field data to develop strategies and objectives 
that guide concept planning.  
Following annexation of the lands, upcoming work will in clude a Strategic Environmen tal Assessment (SEA) to 
establish high level environmental planning objectives and then an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) of the 
proposed neighbourhood plan to assess the effects and ident ify required impact mitigation measures. These two 
environmental management tools are described below. 
Strategic Environmental Assessment  
 Establish and explore a set of high-level strategic decisions about environmental management of the site. 
 Compile the environmental knowledge of the site, in cluding evaluation of field data, to inform the 
consideration of options available to the neighbourhood planning. 
 Develop high-level planning objectives that describe strategies for designing an environmentally responsible 
development plan. 
 Present the results in a SEA report for review by  the Town of Ladysmith and public stakeholders. 
The output of the SEA will help to guide more advanced development concepts.  
Environmental Impact Assessment 
As the neighbourhood plan is developed, an EIA can be conducted that builds on the objectives established in the 
SEA and provides further detailed environmental management by: 
 Describing the elements of the neighbourhood plan that may cause environmental effects of concern. 
 Describing the environmental values of concern. 
 Evaluating and characterizing the environm ental effects of the neighbourhood plan. 
 Prescribing the detailed measures required to mitigate or offset potential impacts to environmental values. 
 Prescribe any required monitoring plans requir ed to verify the success of impact management. 
An EIA is an iterative process that occurs through the preparation of the neighbourhood plan and results in an EIA 
report that includes the environmental commitments required to undertake a devel opment without significant 
effects.

CONCLUSIONS  
During the first phase of environmental planning, we have determined that there are general areas that should be 
prioritized for protection through the development planning process. These areas are presented in the preliminary 
environmental sensitivities map and largely comprise the va lued habitats within riparian areas, mature forest, and 
other rich ecosystems. PGL will continue to work with Couverdon and Sasaki to help preserve and enhance these 
priority areas within the development plan. We will aim to ensure t hat the key environmental values are sustained 
in the area by: 
 Providing ongoing guidance that is based on a thoro ugh understanding of the valued habitats and species on 
the site; 
 Developing further environmental pl anning objectives as part of a SEA; 
 Developing and implementing detailed protection measures  that meet or exceed standard best practices 
(e.g., provincial Develop With Care guidelines) as part of an EIA; and 
 Including priority habitat enhancement in the neighbourhood plan wherever feasible. 
This preliminary work has established a basic understanding of the environmental values for the site. Through the 
environmental planning process described above, we believe  that environmentally re sponsible land development 
is achievable for this property with the continued implementation of diligent environmental practices.
