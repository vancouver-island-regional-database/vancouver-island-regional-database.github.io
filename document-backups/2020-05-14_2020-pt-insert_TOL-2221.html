In 2020, Ladysmith residents will see a slight increase in municipal 
property taxes as we balance a signi ﬁ cant loss of revenue due to 
the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic with capital projects and operating 
expenditures that position the community for a strong economic 
recovery in the coming months. 
We understand that many residents have experienced sudden ﬁ nancial 
hardship in recent months. For this reason, the due date for payments 
remains at July 2, 2020, however, penalties on unpaid property taxes 
will be applied on balances remaining as of 4:00 p.m. on September 30, 
2020. 
This year, residential municipal taxes will rise 1.92 per cent based on the 
assessed value of your property as determined by BC Assessment.  The 
T own is does not have control over tax rates set by other jurisdictions 
such as Vancouver Island Regional Library, Cowichan Valley Regional 
District, Cowichan Valley Regional Hospital.
Residents are still encouraged to try and pay their property taxes by the 
previous July 2 due date, if they can, to help with the T own’s cash ﬂ ow 
as we continue delivering services to the community. Please note that 
a penalty of 10 per cent will be applied to unpaid property taxes and/or 
unclaimed Homeowner Grant at 4:00 on September 30.
Your property taxes help to cover the ongoing costs of roadwork, water 
and sewer projects, other infrastructure improvements, maintaining 
parks, keeping our community safe, supporting many local community 
organizations through Grants-in-Aid, and much more. 
There are a number of projects already underway and steadily moving 
towards completion, or planned for our community in 2020, including:
• Commissioning the T own’s Water Filtration Plant
• Moving Waterfront Area Plan initiatives forward such as the Arts
& Heritage Hub
• Sidewalk upgrades on Buller Street
• Upgrades at our recreational facilities, including F JCC and Forrest
Field
• Renovations and safety upgrades at the Machine Shop
The T own has additional operational costs for this year which also factor 
into the overall tax increase. 
Federal and provincial grants help to offset some of our capital costs and 
reduce the burden on our taxpayers and we will continue to apply for 
various opportunities.  
The T own also received over $875,000 in grant funds earlier this year 
from the Union of BC Municipalities as part of a joint effort with the 
Boys & Girls Club of Central Vancouver Island. 
You may also have noticed the new crosswalk reﬂ ective strips that were 
largely funded by a community grant from ICBC, helping to improve 
pedestrian safety at street crossings all over town and around our 
schools. 
Information about the budget is available on our website at 
www.ladysmith.ca, and at City Hall. We encourage you to review the 
details and direct comments or questions to Council or 
staff.

WHA T SERVICES DO YOUR PROPERTY T AXES PA Y FOR?
Each dollar that you pay in property taxes funds a variety of programs and services. These include:
• Maintaining 900,000 square metres of park land and 26 kilometres of trails
• Caring for 38 kilometres of Ladysmith sidewalks
• Filling 50-100 potholes per year
• Placing approximately 250-300 tons of salt and 200 tons of sand on roads each winter
• Cleaning and repairing 68 kilometres of sanitary sewer lines
• Maintaining a system that supplies 1,407,000,000 litres of clean, safe drinking water
• Maintaining 67.5 kilometres of roads and 17 municipally owned buildings

DID YOU KNOW?
The T own of Ladysmith is only responsible for 
determining a portion of your overall property taxes. 
Altogether, 42.23 per cent of your bill is set by other 
agencies to pay for services like schools, the Vancouver 
Island Regional Library, the Cowichan Valley Regional 
District and the Cowichan Regional Hospital fund.  
Sewer and water parcel taxes make up 20.74 per cent 
of taxes we collect and help to ensure the T own’s 
infrastructure is properly maintained.
