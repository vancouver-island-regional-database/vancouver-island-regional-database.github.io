2025 BUDGET HIGHLIGHTS Ladysmith Council is continuing to prioritize projects that renew aging infrastructure, upgrade public facilities and improve the high level of service delivery that property owners have come to expect. The Town’s and Policing taxes will rise 3.1 per cent for an average residential property, based on the assessed value of your property as determined by BC Assessment. The Town does not have control over tax rates set by other jurisdictions such as Vancouver Island Regional Library, School District 68, Cowichan Valley Regional District (CVRD), or the Cowichan Valley Regional Hospital. The T own is also one of nine regionally significant facilities in the CVRD regional recreation funding model. Taxpayers contribute to the CVRD for the nine facilities and receive user fees for the region. The CVRD compiles the budgets for these facilities and determines the requisition amount for each based on usage. The capital projects to be completed this year align with Council’s Strategic P riorities for Core Infrastructure, Official Community Plan Implementation, Waterfront Area Plan, Economy and Leadership. A number of the significant projects are within our water utility and include: • 6th Ave design for reconstruction • Symonds Street - Road & drainage upgrade • Sewer main upgrade 4th Ave extension • Stocking Lake Dam replacement preparation Other significant projects include: • Various new sidewalks • Various paving projects • Machine Shop artist studios • For rest Field phase II including fitness equipment, pickleball courts, and other amenities • Zoning changes to implement Provincial Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) legislation, allowing some property owners to build up to four units on single-family or duplex zoned properties Federal and Provincial gr ants also help to offset some of our capital costs and reduce the burden on our tax payers. We will continue to apply for various opportunities that align with Council’s strategic priorities. Information about the 2025 budget is available on our website at www.ladysmith.ca. WHAT SERVICES DO YOUR PROPERTY TAXES PAY FOR? Each dollar that you pay in property taxes funds a variety of programs and services. These include: • Maintaining 223 acres of park land and 26 kilometres of trails • Caring for 40.8 kilometres of Ladysmith sidewalks • Placing approximately 250-300 tons of salt and 200 tons of sand on roads each winter • Cleaning and repairing 88.7 kilometres of sanitary sewer lines • Maintaining a system that treats 1,491,884,000L of drinking water and 1,210,687,000L of wastewater • Maintaining 68.1 kilometres of roads and 58 municipally owned buildings and structures The chart below shows how one dollar of your property taxes is spent. In addition to the amounts we’ve listed, property owners also pay water and sewer parcel taxes to help fund upgrades to our water supply system, including the Waste Water Treatment Plant, the Bio-solids Composting Facility, and our Water Filtration Plant. The Town of Ladysmith is only responsible for determining a portion of your overall property taxes. Sewer and water parcel taxes make up 16 per cent of taxes we collect and help to ensure the Town’s infrastructure is properly maintained.
