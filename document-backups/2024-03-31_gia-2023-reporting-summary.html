2024 Ladysmith Festival of Lights
The Ladysmith Festival of Lights is a cherished holiday tradition that illuminates the town, fosters
community spirit, and bolsters local businesses. At its heart is the spectacular "Light Up Night," where
Santa Claus officially switches on the lights.
Beyond the main event the FOL Society supports other groups such as the Magical Christmas LIGHT UP
Tour, inviting participants to explore beautifully decorated homes and areas and vote for their favorites.
This festive celebration strengthens social bonds through volunteer opportunities, local performances,
and a platform for businesses to enhance their visibility and sales during the holiday season. The
Ladysmith Festival of Lights is more than a display—it is a vital part of the community's holiday spirit and
economic vibrancy.
Light Up Night is the centerpiece of the festival, this signature event draws significant crowds, with
attendance often surpassing 20,000 people. This remarkable turnout highlights the event's widespread
appeal and the community's enthusiasm.
In 2024, the Ladysmith Festival of Lights embraced an exciting transformation by introducing several new
elements that brought fresh energy and charm to the event:
• Live Street Entertainment: Replacing the traditional fireworks display, 1st Avenue came alive with
captivating performances by artists with backgrounds in Cirque du Soleil, theater, and opera.
Jugglers, stilt walkers, and an awe-inspiring LED robot circus dazzled the crowds, bringing the magic
of fireworks to street level in an unforgettable way.
• Photo Booths: Two free professional photo booths offered attendees the chance to capture their
festival memories. Guests could choose printed cards or digital copies of their photos, adding a
personalized keepsake to their experience.
• Selfie Photo Arch: Beautifully decorated by the Arts Council, a new selfie photo arch was installed at
the Royal Bank, providing a picture-perfect backdrop for attendees. This whimsical installation, along
with the festival's other decorations, remained in place throughout the season, extending the festive
spirit.
• The festival organizers faced the challenge of adapting to the cancellation of fireworks for the first
time in over two decades due to fire and safety regulations. While this change brought mixed
emotions, it also opened new opportunities. Manyfestivalgoers embraced the fresh and exciting live
entertainment we introduced, though some missed the fireworks that had become a cherished
tradition. We remain committed to working hard to win over all attendees, ensuring that our festival
continues to bring joy, foster connections, and create unforgettable memories for everyone.
We are deeply grateful for the countless sponsors and supporters whose generosity and dedication make
the Ladysmith Festival of Lights possible. Their contributions, whether through time, resources, or
encouragement, light up our community in ways that words cannot fully express. Together, they help us
create a celebration that brings joy, connection, and wonder to everyone who attends.
As we look ahead to 2025, the Ladysmith Festival of Lights remains a beacon of joy, connection, and
pride for our town. With the continued support of the Town Grant, we aim to build on past successes,
introduce new features, and ensure this beloved tradition shines even brighter In the years to come.

Dear Erin Anderson:
This letter is to accompany two documents- our Grant-in-Aid Application and our
Report on Funding for the Grant received from the Town in the past year. The
Ladysmith and District Marine Rescue Society greatly appreciates the support
from the Town and we wish to extend our thanks to the Mayor and Council for
the help received in allowing us to provide marine emergency services in our
waters as well as contribute to water safety and community events throughout
the year. We continue to respond to marine emergencies including sinking and
disabled vessels, persons in the water, searches for missing persons or vessels,
medical and other emergencies and provide support to the ambulance service, .
RCMP, Coast Guard, Land SAR and the coroner. Our volunteers also provide water
safety and community service by safety escort to Yacht Club and Harbour events,
fireworks patrol, safety talks and demonstrations to mariner's groups, and the
children at the Town's summer camp program at Transfer Beach and the
Community Center. We now have six "Kid's Don't Float" PFD loaner stations
installed at marine facilities in the area to keep children safe.

Over this past year, through the Coast Guard Operations Center and a MOD with
the Provincial emergency program, we have found rescue station increasingly
busy with emergency call-outs, including pollution investigations of sunken or
derelict vessels. We now have a lot of catl-outs in the harbour, some of which
have involved sinking vessels or very ill persons or fatalities. We are receiving
requests from RCMP in adjacent areas for assistance with search or recovery
operations and have also transported ambulance service responders to large
anchored vessels in the area when emergencies occurred.
The Society is aware of the Town's revised Grant in Aid Policy which has a new
four year funding arrangement as well as strategic priorities. Our program
provides essential services related to safety and emergency response in the
harbour and waters of the community and thus serves residents, and visitors
alike. Climate change is increasing the severity of weather events and likely will
result in increased emergency response requirements. Maritime activities are a
major economic factor in our area and protecting recreational, commercial and
First Nation boaters is an important priority for our Station and the Community.
We have also recently developed an outreach program with the mills in the
harbour and are working with them to facilitate emergency response to accidents
on the mill sites and log booming grounds in our busy harbour.
We recognize there are many pressing priorities for funding in the community and
greatly appreciate the Town's support for our contributions which are provided
by our highly trained volunteers 24/7 throughout the year.

One to One Support
We meet with caregivers individually to provide confidential support. During a one-to-one support meeting, caregivers
can ask questions, share their feelings, and receive helpful information and resources for their specific caregiving
situation. Support can include assistance navigating the healthcare system, emotional support, referrals to other local
services in the community, and caregiver education resources. Individual support can be provided through in person
meetings, by video call, on the phone, or by email.
Support Groups
Our facilitated support group meetings offer caregivers the opportunity to meet other caregivers, and share their
experiences, feelings, strategies, and helpful resources. These support groups allow caregivers to expand their
community and connect with other people who understand the emotions and unique challenges that caregivers
experience. We offer six in-person support groups per month. Please contact us if you are interested in joining
a support group.
Connection to Community Services
Caregivers need community support. Providing information and referrals to other local services can help caregivers
manage their daily responsibilities and challenges, in the way that best supports their caregiving situation. Various
programs and services are specialized to help caregivers address specific needs. This can include information on
seniors services, financial assistance, food resources, advocacy support, employment support, shelter, and more.
Healthcare System Navigation
A diagnosis can be scary and overwhelming. Many services are available through Island Health. Our staff can help
provide basic information about these services and how to access them. Understanding the healthcare system
empowers caregivers to make decisions and access the support they need.

Finding Peace During The Holiday Season
Slow the pace. The holiday season is not an emergency; there is no race to Christmas Day. Spend time outside.
^ Taking a walk in the crisp winter air can uplift your spirits. Even just stepping outside for a breath of fresh air will
support your nervous system.
Physical activity is a great stress reliever. Find time to move in ways that feel supportive to your body and mental
^^ health. Try gentle types of movement like stretching or dancing in the kitchen. Instead of going for coffee, take
walks with family or friends who are in town for the season.
^
Set healthy boundaries with friends and family. Decide in advance the number of events you are comfortable
attending. It's essential to have some quiet time away from others so you can reconnect with yourself. Look for
moments in your schedule where you can enjoy solitude, even if it's just a 15-minute break to breathe. If you are
feeling lonely, check out the many free community gatherings that are offered throughout December.
The "Holiday Blues" is a real thing. It's that feeling of being sad or overwhelmed during the holidays. The reasons
^^ can vary. The days are shorter, colder, and darker. There is pressure to be joyful when you might not feel that way.
Family gatherings can be stressful. For those with mental health challenges, these feelings can be even stronger.
Awareness of the "Holiday Blues" can help you prepare and take care of yourself. Be gentle with yourself, and remem-
ber that your well-being is the top priority. Make a list of simple things that nourish and uplift you. It's okay if you're
not feeling festive all the time. Take things one step at a time, and know that you CAN ask for help. Research shows
that asking for help can be a meaningful gift to another person.
^ If you're grieving, or if you find yourself walking a hard road right now, be sure to give yourself lots of compassion.
It's ok if this year is not all that you hoped. Expectations set us up for disappointment. Letting go of those expecta-
tionswill help you find a sense of peace. Try to enjoy each moment as it comes. Let go of preconceived ideas of your
ideal holiday gathering. Allow family members and friends the opportunity and grace to just be themselves. This is a gift
we can all give to each other, and ourselves.

Could you please share a little bit about yourself and your caregiving role for our readers?
I was born and raised in Montreal, Quebec. In 1971 I married my high school sweetheart and soul mate, Alma. We had
two beautiful children, and travelled as often as we could when they were young.I have always been comfortable with
change so, in 1999, we moved from our hometown to Calgary. We continued West from there, and we finally settled
and retired in Ladysmith in 2014. I am 75 years old, but have a 7 year old that lives within me. He is full of life and
keeps me focused.
I credit my mom for laying down a foundation of self-sufficiency and autonomy, by teaching me life skills such as
cooking, baking, ironing, sewing, how to do the laundry, making my own decisions, and making mistakes...and having
to solve them on my own. She always taught me to be kind, and to think of others.
After my mom passed away from lung cancer at the age of 55, it took me years to grasp and appreciate her gifts of
strength, love, wisdom, patience, and not to fear death. These gifts have all had a significant impact on my life.
Dementia is familiar to me; I was 49 when I became the sole caregiver for my aunt who had Alzheimer's. I had to
arrange for her to enter a long-term care facility, and I had also took over caring for her two sisters, as she was their
primary caretaker before she developed dementia. Acquiring responsibility for three people at one time had real
challenges, struggles, and everlasting visions pertaining to the cruelty of dementia. My sister lessened the burden by
taking on the caregiver role for one of our aunts.
My wife Alma was diagnosed in mid-2015 with MCI (Mild Cognitive Impairment). The first five years, with the excep-
tion of her anxiety and panic attacks, we had a pretty normal quality of life. Our focus, with our daughter Christina
leading the way, was to research the root cause of dementia, in an attempt to slow both the progression of the dis-
ease, and reduce Alma's anxiety and panic attacks. My goal is to keep her at home to the end of life.
In April of 2016 we expressed interest in participating in a clinical trial at the UBC Hospital for Alzheimer's disease. In
May of that year Alma participated in the pre-screening process but, unfortunately, was not accepted. In an attempt to
continue our fight against the disease we sought the help of a naturopath, and also tried many other things such as;
supplements, hypnosis therapy for anxiety and panic attacks, photo-biomodulation (laser and red light therapy),
ketogenic diet, and so much more, all to no avail. Alma's dementia continued to progress.
In 2017 we decided to try a medication for Alzheimer's that showed some benefits, slowing the progression in certain
studies. But Alma, like many others, did not react well and the side effects outweighed the benefits. Actually, there
were no benefits, unfortunately. Today she is on a drug called Trazodone for anxiety that appears to be somewhat
working.

The last six months have seen a more rapid decline, forcing me to re-evaluate how to best take care of her and what

What helpful habits or practices have you developed to support or prioritize your needs?
Listening to music, active workouts, self-reflection, and constantly reminding myself to not sweat the small stuff, be

compassion.
How does the word 'caregiver' fit for you?
At our wedding the minister made a statement, "today, two shall become one". We are soul mates for life. So my

that often requires us to ask for help.
What is the gift (or what is rewarding) in the caregiving journey for you?
The fact that I am Alma's caregiver, how I can make us feel, and that I am making a difference in the quality of our

How has your caregiving role evolved over time?
It has evolved from being easy at first, to being somewhat challenging, to at times very challenging. Things can change
at any moment depending on the situation. Being flexible and adapting to immediate change, trying to be consistently
tactful, and exhibiting super powers of patience is difficult, when they all occur at the same time,
What do you think of when you hear the words: self-care?
Awareness of who you are, acknowledging your vulnerabilities, your needs, asking for help. Staying strong by taking
: care of yourself; to get through heartbreak, brutally tough, and emotionally tough times. The key for me is to be
proactive in addressing a number of what if s, such as: What if I pass away first? What will my life look like if she is in
long term care? What will my life be like without her? Where will I live? What if I can no longer drive, etc. ?
What kind of self-care do you find essential while caregiving?
Eating properly, physical fitness, staying socially active, self-reflection, and daring myself to be remarkable by creating
moments of joy every day. Those first three things are, at times, the most difficult for me.
Do you have a piece of hard-won wisdom you'd like to share with other caregivers?
Acknowledging my grief (one of the wolves), and that I am slowly losing the love of my life. The reality that I have two
Alma's (and our daughters have two moms). That I need to be on HER journey full-time, in order to mitigate the frus-
tration we were both experiencing. This has proven to work for us. Accepting fibbing as the new norm helps us manage
daily challenges. It makes life significantly easier and less stressful for both of us. Acknowledging my inner struggles (as
part of the two wolves that live within me). One of the wolves is sad, grieving and lonely. The other wolf is happy,
loving and content. The sad wolf fights the happy wolf all of time, and both are a part of this journey.
Can you share a time when some unexpected or useful help came that made a difference for you?
.The special relationships that have developed with friends, past colleagues, and people that just happened into my life.
People who have openly shared their feelings and emotions as caregivers, including losing their spouse and mourning.
All of this resonates with me. Sometimes love hurts and you need to live boldly and push yourself.
Connecting with another caregiver, whose wife is in long-term care, has developed into an awesome friendship. We
share our feelings; we can both shed tears and toss out that myth that real men don't cry. We golf, have
lunch together, we have visited each other's homes and met each other's wife. Our coming together has made and
continues to make a significant difference in each of our lives.
What do you find useful when accessing support from Cowichan Family Caregivers Support Society?
The awesome facilitators; many if not all of whom have been, or are, caregivers themselves. They are passionate,
enthusiastic, professional, positive, and authentic. They understand and easily connect with you.
The Society's awareness of resources helped me make meaningful choices towards future planning. Personally, my
face-to-face meetings with my group facilitator, Lynne, and previously with Amanda, have been significantly valuable
; towards navigating through the system and my personal wellbeing.

Caregiver Coaching Program
Are you feeling overwhelmed in your role as a caregiver? Are you unsure of what to do next or where to go for help?
We have a Caregiver Coaching service, where caregivers are provided free, one-on-one sessions that are designed to
help empower you to find solutions and answers.
A Caregiver Coaching session is beneficial, if:
• You are helping to care for a family member or friend with complex needs.
• You need support and guidance navigating the health care system to ensure your care recipient is receiving the
right type of support at the right time.
• You are ready to take action.
Caregiver Coaching Sessions Include:
• A discovery process to gather information about your caregiving situation. This can be done prior to your session
or right at the beginning, and takes about 10 minutes.
• A call with a Caregiver Support Coach who will assess your needs and help you prioritize areas requiring immediate
support and action.
• Recommendations and tips from your Caregiver Coach to help you generate an action plan, which may include
learning how to navigate the health care system, prioritizing next steps, and strategies to stay healthy while being
busy and under stress.
