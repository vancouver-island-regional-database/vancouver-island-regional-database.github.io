CANNABIS CONSULTATION
Ladysmith residents can watch their mailboxes over 
the coming weeks as we prepare to send out a survey 
regarding the retail sale of cannabis.
The current zoning bylaw prohibits the sale of cannabis 
within the municipality and your feedback will help the 
Town decide where cannabis stores could be located. 
Options will be available for returning a hard copy of the 
survey to City Hall, or completing it electronically.

LADYSMITH TAKES STEPS FOR 
SUSTAINABLE GROWTH
The Town of Ladysmith continues to be discovered as 
a very desirable community to live, work and play and 
as a result has been cited as one of the fastest growing 
communities in British Columbia for last year.
A recent BC Stats report on population estimates shows 
Ladysmith welcomed approximately 324 new residents in 
2018. This 3.6% year-over-year increase makes our town 
the fastest growing municipality among those with a 
population of at least 5,000 residents. 
“The continued investments in making our community 
an attractive place to live and do business are paying 
dividends, ” said Mayor Aaron Stone. “We have become 
one of the premiere destinations for new residents and 
businesses in one of the most in-demand areas of Canada. ”
The Province estimates year-over-year population growth 
by using the health client registry and residential hydro 
hook-ups.  
Ladysmith’s population is currently projected to be 9,417 
and the community has grown an estimated 2.9% annually

BUSINESS FRIENDLY COMMUNITIES

strategy priority.

and the Ladysmith Downtown Business Association.

future generations.
The Open for Business Awards recognize and celebrate 
communities that best demonstrate they are promoting 
business friendly initiatives within their region. 
DCC BYLAW REVIEW UPDATE
The Town of Ladysmith is fi  nalizing the updated De-
velopment Cost Charges (DCC) Bylaw after several in-
depth Council workshops and a consultation process 
with the development community as well as other en-
gaged citizens. 
Development Cost Charges or DCCs are collected from 
developers or builders to help pay for the capital cost 
of installing growth-related infrastructure.
DCCs are collected for water, sanitary sewer, storm 
drainage, roads and park acquisition and parkland 
improvement. DCCs are payable by anyone obtaining 
subdivision approval for the creation of new residen-
tial parcels or a building permit for the construction 
or expansion of commercial, industrial, institutional or 
multi-family residential projects or for a single family 
residential building permit when a DCC has not previ-
ously been paid.
The current DCC Bylaw was adopted in 201 1. Regular 
review is important to ensure that the projects, associ-
ated costs and growth estimates are up-to-date. 
The timeframe for a DCC program is typically 20 years 
The review of the current program started in 2016.
Council received feedback from the DCC Open House 
at a meeting in March and approved the fi  rst three 
readings of the new Bylaw at its meeting on April 1.

TABLETS AND PAPERLESS AGENDAS
In keeping with Ladysmith’s commitment to sustainability, 
Town Council will save more than 50,000 sheets of paper 
each year with its switch to paperless agendas. 
Ladysmith joins the local governments across Canada 
that are replacing often lengthy paper-based meeting 
documents with digital versions which can be accessed 
at a Council meeting through a secure Microsoft Cloud-
based platform called eScribe. 
If you’re attending a Council meeting or watching it from 
the comfort of your home, you’ll see Council members 
referencing their tablets for agenda material. 
Councillors will also be able to add comments and follow 
up notes using their tablets in the same way they would 
with a hard copy version.
