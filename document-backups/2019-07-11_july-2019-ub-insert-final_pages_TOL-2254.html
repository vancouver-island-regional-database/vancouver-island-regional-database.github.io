Stage 3 Water Restrictions remain in effect for 
residents of Ladysmith, the Diamond Improvement 
District and Stz’uminus First Nation as together we 
remain concious about our water usage. 
The T own of Ladysmith took this proactive step on 
June 21 as a result of extremely low water ﬂ ows in 
our local creeks as well as the warm temperatures 
expected over the coming months.
Since late June we have received some precipitation, 
and while there is not a water shortage, the public’s 
role in conservation is still vital due to the increased 
risk of wild ﬁ res across Vancouver Island. 
Ladysmith currently averages 5,000 cubic metres

to 4,000 cubic metres would help to keep our water 
supply levels sustainable.
Every drop counts, and this year more than ever, as we 
enter Stage 3 Water Restrictions almost two months 
earlier than in 2018.
Throughout the spring, the community experienced 
rainfall totals below the recent historical average 
which subsequently led to less water ﬂ owing through 
our streams.

CURBSIDE RECYCLING
Have you ever experienced the satisfaction of 
collecting a tall stack of ﬂ yers into a plastic bag 
and throwing it in with the curbside recycling? 
Chances are you’ve just contaminated your recycling 
altogether. 
In fact, plastic bags are among the top sources of 
frustration when it comes to our local contaminated 
curbside recycling. 
In October 2017, the T own was ﬁ rst informed by 
Recycle BC that the contamination rate of our 
curbside recycling stream had reached a high level. 
Contamination means your recycling contains 
material collected that is not acceptable in the 
Recycle BC curbside collection program. 
These materials can limit how effectively recycling 
material is processed and present health and safety 
risks for both collection and post-collection staff. 
Ladysmith’s curbside contaminants include, in 
order of prominence: 
1. Non-PPP – Non-recyclable garbage and 
product residue (e.g. paper napkins, single use 
plastics and forms of packaging); 
2. Unsortable materials - materials which are 
unable to be sorted and recycled due to being 
tied up in plastic bags, stuffed/nested together 
etc.;
3. Scrap metal (e.g. keys, nails, cooking pots, 
hangers); 
4. Multi–laminates (such as zipper-top pouches, 
granola packaging, frozen vegetable bags, and 
so on); 
5. Glass.
Ladysmith residents have a role to play in making 
our community a leader in sustainability.
Most of the items that are contributing to 
contaminated recycling levels in Ladysmith can be 
dropped off for free at the Peerless Road Recycling 
Centre. 
Download Recycle BC’s recycling link app to ﬁ nd 
out what recyclable material goes where, check 
your collection day, ﬁ nd your nearest depot location 
and receive noti ﬁ cations about your recycling pick-
up.  Or go to: https://www.cvrd.bc.ca/recyclopedia
for a list of where and how to dispose of items for 
disposal.
