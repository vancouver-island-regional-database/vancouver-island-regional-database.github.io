1.Introduction: Ben welcomed everyone and gave a land acknowledgement.   
2.Meeting Intent: Ben noted that the intent of the meeting was to give the Steering Committee members in 
attendance an update on the work that has been done since the previous Steering Committee meeting.  
3.Meetings with Elders Update: Ben gave a short summary of the meetings that have been held with the 
Stz’uminus Elders. It was noted that the project team is very fortunate to have the involvement of the Elders, and 
their input has been very valuable. It was noted that the longhouse/big house is an important element of 
Stz’uminus culture, and as such elements of longhouses/big houses will likely be incorporated into the project.

4.Project Organization and Structure: Ben reviewed how the Artists Studio building and associated public 
space are a component of the Ladysmith Arts and Heritage Hub, which itself is a component of the Waterfront 
Area Plan. He also reviewed how design information for the Artists Studio building will ﬁrst be reviewed by the 
Stz’uminus Elders before it is disseminated to the Steering Committee to ensure it is an appropriate expression 
of Stz’uminus Culture. 
5.Cultural Elements in the Artists Studio Building: The Steering Committee noted the Artists Studio building 
seemed to be incorporating elements typically included in cultural centres. They agreed that the building program 
has evolved since the project inception, and the incorporation of cultural elements is a positive step for the 
design. John agreed, and noted that Stz’uminus culture is intrinsic to art, and that it is good to remain open to a 
number of inﬂuences. Ben also noted that one of the goals of the design team is to provide a building which acts 
as an ‘open framework’ that can accommodate a number of activities to help ensure long term sustainability.
6.Funding Application Wording: ToL will share the funding applications with the design team and Steering 
Committee. 
7.Landscaping and Public Space Design: The project landscape and public space design was discussed. 
-The landscape and public space design will be challenging, but represents an opportunity for the 
project. 
-The landscape design can create a ‘non-linear’ narrative of the overlapping histories of the site 
-This project is important for the implementation of Truth and Reconciliation. As such, there may be 
areas that are for use by members of the Stz’uminus community only. 
-Permeability for both the building and the landscaping will be an important consideration moving 
forward. 
-Some aspects of the site history are painful for members of the Stz’uminus community, the design team 
will work to ensure that the site history is expressed in an honest and constructive manner.  
-The public space design will need to maintain a balance of uses. The ToL envisions this space as a 
place for the public to gather, and as a meeting ground for members of the Ladysmith and Stz’uminus 
communities. The meeting/collaboration of Ladysmith and Stz’uminus community members was an 
integral component of the funding application, and must be maintained.
8.Rails and Rolling Stock: The on-site rails and rolling stock were discussed:
-The Historical Society is preparing proposals related to the rails and rolling stock. They will forward 
these proposals to the design team.  
-The rolling stock currently on site would be difﬁcult to store anywhere off-site. 
-The design team will review the presentations given by the Historical Society and Maritime Society at 
the ﬁrst Steering Committee meeting. They will also coordinate a site visit with the Historical Society as 
soon as it is safe to do so. 
9.Oyster Bay Drive: Oyster Bay Drive road discussed:
-CPA will coordinate a meeting with Ryan Bouma (ToL), the civil consultant and PFS to review the design 
requirements for Oyster Bay Drive.  
-The road and public space design will need to ensure the Artists Studio building is not cut off from the 
rest of the site by Oyster Bay Drive, and there are no pedestrian/vehicular conﬂicts. 
-The Waterfront Area Plan includes plans for a through-road to be located below the project site. The 
truck trafﬁc currently running on Oyster Bay Drive could be shifted to this proposed through-road, which 
would enable the space in front of the Artists Studio building to have a more “plaza-like” feel which 
favours pedestrian circulation.
