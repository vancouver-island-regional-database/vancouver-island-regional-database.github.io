RECOMMENDED ENFORCEMENT ACTIONS: Section 57 – Notice on Title The Town’s Building Inspectors are responsible for ensuring that all new buildings and renovations comply with the Town’s bylaws and the BC Building Code. The purpose of these regulations are to ensure buildings are durable and safe for the intended use. Where work is completed without required permits and inspections, significant deficiencies can occur that pose a risk of injury or death, shorten the lifespan of buildings, or make them unusable for their intended purpose. Tenants or future owners who may be unaware of these conditions are often the most significantly affected. A Notice on Title under section 57 of the Community Charter, is an enforcement tool used by local governments throughout British Columbia to alert interested parties to building infractions on a particular property. Once filed, the notice appears on all title searches under “Legal Notations,” and parties with an interest in the land (e.g., realtors, lenders, buyers, lawyers or any member of the public wishing to obtain a title search from the Land Titles Office) will become aware a contravention has occurred and can inquire with the local government about the nature of the contravention. A Notice on Title may be registered on title when: a) A building inspector observes a condition resulting from a contravention of a municipal bylaw, the BC Building Code or another applicable enactment, and concludes that, “a building or other structure is unsafe or is unlikely to be usable for its intended purpose during its normal lifetime,” or b) A building inspector determines that work was completed without a required permit or a satisfactorily completed inspection. Under Section 57(3) of the Community Charter, Council must pass a resolution directing the Corporate Officer to file a Notice on Title and only after providing the property owner and Building Inspector an opportunity to be heard. If Council confirms the recommendations of the Building Inspector, Council must pass a resolution directing the Corporate Officer to file a notice in the Land Title Office. The notice must state that a resolution has been passed pursuant to Section 57 of the Community Charter and that additional information is available at City Hall. Although a Notice on Title can be ordered by Council for the mere absence of a permit or inspection, staff generally reserve recommending this tool for “high risk” or serious infractions. Although the space appears to be used for storage consistent with a typical residential garage, an unverified structural condition presents a safety risk regardless of use . Due to the Town’s inability to inspect these elements retrospectively, detailed assessment s by a qualified structural engineer and geotechnical engineer are necessary to determine whether the structure will perform safely as per the requirements of the Building Code. Building Code The front roof overhang spans a considerable distance and appears to exceed typical prescriptive Building Code limits for non-engineered construction. While an engineered Laminated Veneer Lumber (LVL) beam has been installed, staff have not received engineered design drawings or manufacturer specifications confirming that the beam size, span, bearing, and connections are adequate. In the absence of this information, staff cannot verify whether the overhang is properly supported or whether it may be subject to excessive deflection or failure. If the LVL is over-spanned, improperly installed, or lacking required structural hardware, there is a potential risk of roof collapse or roof uplift from wind or snow loading conditions. In addition, because the building’s ability to withstand seismic loading has not been confirmed, the risks associated with both a potentially over-spanned roof and seismic failure pose a direct life-safety concern for anyone in or around the building. Generally, municipalities have no legal duty to enforce bylaws when enforcement is not mandated by statute and there is no such mandated duty with respect to building bylaws. However, a municipality that has adopted a building bylaw owes a duty of care to those who might be injured by the negligent exercise of those powers such as failing to enforce known and significant contraventions of the building bylaw. This duty of care may be met by issuing warnings such as a Section 57 Notice. In short, when building contraventions are known, the Town has a duty to either enforce or ensure that those at risk are made aware of the contraventions; the Town may be liable if neither action is taken. Although it has consequences for the owner (such as creating a deterrent for future buyers), filing the notice is intended to be a less punitive action. It serves as a disclosure mechanism to protect future owners and other parties with an interest in the property. No further action is required by Council once the Notice is registered on title and the Town will have met its duty of care for the less urgent bylaw infractions. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: This report recommends replacing current fire suppression equipment to standardize hose and nozzle pairing, improving operational safety and fireground effectiveness. Adopting a standardized pairing will help reduce hose kinks, lower operating pressures, and improve overall water flow performance. Overall, this change will support more efficient operations and better outcomes during emergency response. PREVIOUS COUNCIL DIRECTION: N/A INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND: Staff have reviewed Ladysmith Fire/Rescue operations and identified deficiencies in existing firefighting equipment. The high-pressure fog nozzles currently in use are outdated, increasingly unreliable, and not aligned with modern firefighting standards or expected fire flows. These nozzles are rated at a maximum of 125 gallons per minute (gpm) at 100 psi and are unable to consistently achieve the 300 gpm combined flow recommended by the National Fire Protection Association for initial attack lines. The existing hose is designed for high-pressure operation and relies on back pressure to prevent kinking. This results in increased nozzle reaction, making hose lines more difficult to manage and contributing to firefighter fatigue. Staff propose transitioning to lower-pressure (50 psi), higher-flow (165 gpm) smooth bore nozzles, paired with hose designed for higher flows at lower pressures. This standardized replacement will improve operational performance and streamline maintenance by reducing the number of equipment models requiring inventory and repair. Page 59 of 64
