PUBLIC HEARING Town of Ladysmith Zoning Bylaw 1995, No. 1160, Amendment 
Bylaw (No. 97) 2014, No. 1848 – 1144 Rocky Creek Road 
 
  
 
 
 
 
 
   
Members of the public present:  24 
 
Mayor Hutchins explained that t his Public Hearing is for a Zoning 
Bylaw Amendment – Application 3360-14-01. 
 
The subject property is Lot A, District Lot 38, Oyster District, Plan 
EPP23810.  The property is located at 1144 Rocky Creek Road. 
 
Director of Development Services, Felicity Adams, explained that 


 
Town of Ladysmith Regular Council Meeting Minutes: March 17, 2014  Page 2 
the subject of the Public Hearing is: 
• Town of Ladysmith Zoning Bylaw 1995, No. 1160, Amendment 
Bylaw (No. 97), 2014, No. 1848   
 
Bylaw 1848 proposes to amend the Zoning Bylaw by: 
• Placing the ‘Light Industrial Zone (I-1) on the subject property. 
 
Covenant Discharge: 
At the time of the previous rezoning to I1 -A Zone, a covenant was 
registered on title of the properties being rezoned to I1 -A Zone to 
collect an amenity contribution of $1000 per residential unit.  The 
subject property was part of that earlier rezoning application.  This 
covenant is not applicable to the subject rezoning as additional 
residential density is not part of this proposal and, as such, it would 
be discharged from the title of the subject property as part of the 
approval of this rezoning application. 
 
The Public Hearing notice  was printed in the Chronicle newspaper 
on March 4, 2014 and March 11, 2014 and posted on community 
notice boards throughout Town, as well as on the Town’s website. A 
copy of the Notice was made available at the front c ounter at City 
Hall for the two week notice period.    
 
No written submission s and one telephone or in -person enquiry  
have been received at City Hall in connection with this Public 
Hearing. 
 
Mayor Hutchins called for comments by a representative of the 
applicant, Densen Investments Inc.  There was no applicant 
representative present. 
 
Mayor Hutchins called three times for questions about the Bylaw 
Amendments.  There were no questions.   
 
Mayor Hutchins called three times for comments and written 
submissions about the Bylaw Amendments .  No comments or 
written submissions were provided.     
     
Hearing no questions and receiving no comments or written 
submissions, Mayor Hutchins declared the Public Hearing for town 
of Ladysmith Bylaw 1848 closed, explaining that Council may not 
receive further submissions on the bylaw , and that Council may, 
without further notice or hearing, 
(a) adopt or defeat the bylaw, or 
(b) alter and then adopt the bylaw, provided that the alteration 
does not 
(i)  alter the use, 
(ii)  increase the density, or 
(iii)  without the owner’s consent, decrease the density of any 
area from that originally specified in the bylaw.

Ladysmith Resources Centre Association – Grants-in-Aid Requests 
The following representatives of the Ladysmith Resources Centre 
Association made presentations about their programs and services 
for residents of Ladysmith and area to support the grants -in-aid 
requests from the Ladysmith Resources Centre Association: 
 
Dennis Lait – LRCA overview and General Programming 
Carla Sampson – Victim Services 
Miaike Lammel – Family Support Services 
Lindsay Paaauwe – Youth at Risk 
Tanya Reid –  Adventures in Literacy  and Parent -Child Mother 
Goose 
Dale Gisbourne – Dad’s Group 
 
Council was advised that approximately 40 per cent of clients of 
these programs reside in the Ladysmith area but outside the Town 
boundaries.  Council members considered ways to seek funding 
support from Cowichan Valley Regional District Areas G and H and 
encouraged LRCA representatives to continue to request funding  
directly from CVRD Areas G and H, based on  program information 
and client statistics. 
 
Ladysmith Food Bank Community Kitchen Project 
Natasha Sharpe of the Ladysmith Food Bank provided council with 
an overview of the Ladysmith Community Kitchen initiative.  The 
organization is requesting a $2,000 grant -in-aid from Council in 
2014. 
 
Cowichan Therapeutic Riding Association 
Staff advised that there are 10 clients and 10 volunteers from the

NEW BUSINESS  Stz’uminus First Nation Challenge 
 
 
 
 
Council discussed  a challenge from the Stz’uminus First Nation, 
and options for responding to the challenge.  A member of the 
audience advised Council that it has the option to respond to the 
challenge by ‘paying it forward’.  It was agreed that Council would 
respond to the Stz’uminus First Nation challenge by ‘paying it 
forward’.  Two ideas put forward were to have council members 
cook and/or serve at the Ladysmith community Kitchen or the 
Ladysmith Seafood Festival.  Councillors Dashwood and Horth will 
investigate options and report back to Council.
