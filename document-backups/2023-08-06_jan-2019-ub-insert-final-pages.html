PUBLIC ART STRATEGY
Whether it’s the many historical artifacts found 
around town, or the Salish Wind canoe at Transfer 
Beach Park,  our green open spaces, streets and 
downtown alleyways are full of so many community 
treasures in the form of public art. 
In early September, the Arts Council of Ladysmith & 
District was awarded a contract by the Town to 
complete a Public Art Strategy. 
Local residents from both Ladysmith and Stz’uminus 
First Nation responded enthusiastically to the idea 
by engaging in public presentations, workshops, 
walking tours, surveys and a post card project to 
garner their feedback on current examples of public art 
and how we can better reimagine our public spaces.  
The idea for a Public Art Strategy was born out of 
Ladysmith’s 2016 Parks and Recreation Master Plan with 
the goal of developing guiding principles and implemen-
tation steps for more accessible forms of artistry in the 
community. 
The completed draft Public Art Strategy, including a set 
of recommendations,  will be reviewed in January by the 
Municipal Services Committee and Parks, Recreation & 
Culture Advisory Committee before going to Council.
Thank you for being a part of the process.

SNOW AND ICE
What happens when it snows in Ladysmith
The mountains overlooking Ladysmith may be 
blanketed with snow but those toboggans and shovels 
are still collecting dust in our garages.  It’s still a good 
idea to be prepared to deal with snow on our streets.
In the event of a snowfall, the ﬁ rst priority for the 
plows is to clear access to public buildings such as 
the Community Health Centre, RCMP detachment, 
Ambulance Station and Fire/Rescue Station.   
We then clear and maintain all main roads on a 24 
hour basis as the snow continues to fall.  Our primary 
concern is keeping the main roadways, First Avenue 
Fourth Avenue, Dogwood Drive, Davis Road and 
Chemainus Road open to trafﬁ c. 
Weather permitting, we then clear snow from the main 
hills throughout the Town, focusing on widening the 
roads we have already cleared to make room for trafﬁ c.  
Local neighbourhood roads will be cleared after the 
main thoroughfares (listed above) are completed. 
Through roads will be cleared ﬁ rst, and then cul-de-
sacs.  Did you know it can take about 48 hours to clear 
all 60 kilometres of roads in Ladysmith?
Remember that it is your responsibility to shovel the 
sidewalk in front of your house by 10:00 am after a 
snowfall.  This helps to ensure the safety of your
neighbours and other pedestrians.  We also provide a 
free  salt/sand mixture at the Public Works Yard.

LOT 5 EXPROPRIATION
Offer to purchase property
Last year, the previous Town of Ladysmith Council 
directed staff to acquire a 3.2 acre property (Lot 5) to 
be included in our municipal parks and open spaces, as 
outlined in the Waterfront Area Plan (WAP).  
The Town made an offer to purchase Lot 5 at market 
value, but the owners rejected the offer. The Town 
then proceeded to expropriate the land as authorized 
under the 
Community Charter and Expropriation Act.
A local government can acquire a property when it’s 
in the public interest to do so but we are required to 
follow this legislated process. 
Lot 5 is predominantly a rock scarp face covered with 
mature trees and portions of well-used pedestrian 
trails surrounded by provincial and town lands.

WATER MAIN FLUSHING PROGRAM
Regular water main ﬂushing is an important part of a comprehensive water management program. Each year, 
between January and April, the T own’s Utilities Department ﬂushes both water supply mains and distribution water 
mains. Potable water is ﬂushed at a high velocity through the water mains to ensure all piping is refreshed and minor 
sediment within the pipes is removed. This is one of the methods relied upon by the T own to help keep our drinking 
water safe and clean.
During the ﬂ ushing period, water users may experience some discolouration of the water supply. Running 
a cold tap for a short period will help restore water quality back to normal. If you or someone you know depends on 
medical equipment that uses water, please call Public Works at 250-245-6445 for ﬂushing dates.
Yearly water main ﬂushing is also a reminder to clean the screens on your faucets as well as the pressure reducing 
valve (where water enters the house). This should be done on an annual basis. Thank you for your patience and 
cooperation!
