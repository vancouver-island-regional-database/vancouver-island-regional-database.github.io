For Immediate Release 
 
STAGE 3 WATER RESTRICTIONS NOW IN EFFECT 
LADYSMITH, July 7, 2015–Effective immediately, the Town of Ladysmith, Diamond Improvement 
District, and Stz’uminus First Nation lands are all under Stage 3 water restrictions.  The rest of the 
Cowichan Valley Regional District will move to Stage 3 restrictions tomorrow (July 8). 
 Several factors prompted the decision to move to Stage 3 water restrictions as a proactive, 
precautionary measure.  The unusually hot, dry weather in May and June created excess 
evaporation in Holland and Stocking Lakes, and the forecast for the rest of the summer indicates 
continued hot, dry weather.  The Town also recorded high water demands for the month of June.  In 
addition, it is important to ensure that there is enough water on hand in the face of the current fire 
hazard conditions in the area.  The recent announcement by the provincial government that 
Vancouver Island is at Drought Level 4 – the highest level – confirmed that implementing Stage 3 
water restrictions is the right decision. 
 Although the water level in both Stocking and Holland Lakes was down by about 20 per cent 
at the end of June, the Town still has sufficient reserves to provide over 200 days of water at the 
usage levels recorded in June.  The anticipated reduction of water use under Stage 3 restrictions 
will extend that reserve. 
 During Stage 3 water restrictions, the following apply: 
• NO Lawn Watering (using sprinklers and irrigation systems)  
• NO filling pools and hot tubs.  However, in order to avoid damage to pumps and machinery, you 
may top up your hot tubs and pools to make up for evaporation. 
• NO washing vehicles, houses, boats , driveways and sidewalks. Limited washing of driveways 
and houses is allowed as part of the preparation for applying paints or preservatives, or pouring 
concrete.   
• Hand Watering of trees, shrubs and gardens using a hose with a spring-loaded nozzle, a bucket 
or watering can is allowed for a maximum of 2 hours a day, between 6:00 and 8:00 a.m. or 
8:00 and 10:00 p.m.
  

 
 
• Micro-drip irrigation can take place at any time, for a maximum of 4 hours a day.  Please note 
that micro- or drip irrigation uses less than 20 gallons of water per hour at less than 25 pounds 
per square inch (psi).  This does NOT include soaker or weeper hoses. 
 “I know my lawn will just lie dormant, not die, and that it will turn green again when it rains,” 
said Mayor Aaron Stone. “There are many other ways we can all do our part to save water, over and 
above the Stage 3 Restrictions, such as saving bath and cooking water to use on our gardens, or 
turning off taps when brushing teeth and washing hands. Although we have good water supply, it is 
important that we use this opportunity to change the way we look at water use and do all that we 
can to protect our most valuable resource.” 
 The Town has already implemented a number of practices to reduce water use.  Wherever 
possible and practical, Town park lawns, playing fields and the cemetery are either not being 
watered, or are receiving the minimum permitted water to maintain a healthy root system.  
Vegetable and flower beds are watered daily, while street trees are watered twice a week with a 
water bag.  In addition, the Spray Park at Transfer Beach will now be on reduced hours, from 11:30 
a.m. to 6:30 p.m. daily. 
 Water-wise tips for residents include: 
• Install a low flow toilet (and apply for a $75 rebate from the Town) 
• Turn off the tap when brushing teeth, washing hands, cleaning vegetables, washing dishes, 
shaving… 
• While running the tap to get hot water, save the cold water for another use 
• Keep a pitcher of drinking water in the refrigerator instead of running the tap 
• Take short showers instead of baths, and switch to low-flow or aerated shower heads and taps.  
Turn off the shower to lather, then turn it back on to rinse.  Shortening a shower by a minute or 
two can save about 150 gallons a month 
• Run full loads in the washing machine and dishwasher.  An energy star dishwasher uses less 
water than washing dishes by hand 
• Choose drought-resistant and native plant species 
• Collect rainwater in a barrel and use it for watering 
• Use mulch on gardens to hold water 
• Weed beds regularly — weeds compete with plants and vegetables for water 
• Cover swimming pools and hot tubs when not in use to reduce evaporation 

 
 
• Run decorative fountains only when you are there to enjoy them 
• Use porous materials on walkways, driveways and patios to help absorb water and prevent 
wasteful run-off
