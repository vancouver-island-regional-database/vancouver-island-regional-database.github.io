Why contribute?

Access is more than just availability

Public information can become difficult to access when websites change, files are removed, systems are replaced, access requires a fee, or the same document must be requested repeatedly.

Sharing documents that have already been obtained can help preserve access and reduce unnecessary duplication of complex retrieval processes.

Freedom of Information / Access to Information releases Paid public records Archived copies Removed documents Historical versions Related government records

Documents should have been obtained lawfully and are reviewed before publication.

Open-government practice

Sharing previously released documents is already part of open government

Public-access systems already recognize that information released for one request can be useful to other people. Provincial and federal open-government services make previously released or completed access-to-information records easier to find and reuse.

The same accessibility principle can be applied at the community level by preserving and organizing relevant documents that members of the public have already obtained.

Open data is defined as structured data that is machine-readable, freely shared, used and built on without restrictions. The key things to remember about open data are:

  • Availability and access: the data must be available as a whole and at no more than a reasonable reproduction cost, preferably by downloading over the internet. The data must also be available in a convenient and modifiable form.
  • Re-use and redistribution: the data must be provided under terms that permit re-use and redistribution including the intermixing with other datasets.
  • Universal participation: everyone must be able to use, re-use and redistribute. There should be no discrimination against fields of endeavour or against persons or groups. For example, 'non-commercial' restrictions that would prevent 'commercial' use, or restrictions of use for certain purposes (e.g. only in education), are not allowed.
Open Data Definition, Government of Canada — Open Government Portal
