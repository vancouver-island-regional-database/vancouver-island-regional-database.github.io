You have the right to access information about your own government

Access to government information is protected in Canada as a fundamental right because meaningful public debate and free expression on matters of public governance depend on it. But accessibility is not just about availability. It is also about removing barriers like technology, terminology, location, education, and cost, so that all citizens can participate fully and equally in the democratic process. ⓘAccess to Information Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. A-1, s. 2(2)(a); Accessible British Columbia Act, SBC 2021, c 19, s 2(1).

Vancouver Island Regional Database is an easy-to-use, centralized online toolkit for finding, understanding, and analyzing public records across jurisdictions and institutions in the Mid-Island region. Created by the public, for the public.*

ⓘ Access to Information Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. A-1, s. 2(2)(a); Accessible British Columbia Act, SBC 2021, c 19, s 2(1).
*VIRD is an independent, citizen-led initiative not affiliated with any government agency, corporate sponsor, or political faction.

The overarching purpose of access to information legislation … is to facilitate democracy. It does so in two related ways. It helps to ensure first, that citizens have the information required to participate meaningfully in the democratic process, and secondly, that politicians and bureaucrats remain accountable to the citizenry.

Gerard LaForest, former Supreme Court of Canada Justice, Dagg v. Canada (1997)
